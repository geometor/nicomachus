=========================================================================
Chapter 17: Heteromecic, Promecic, Circular & Spherical Numbers — Summary
=========================================================================

.. folio:: introductio_arithmetica:0271
   :page: p. 255

Overview
--------

Nicomachus distinguishes *heteromecic* from *promecic* rectangles, links squares/heteromecics to the cosmic principles of Same and Other, and completes his taxonomy of solids:

1. **Heteromecic vs. Promecic (II.17.1–2)**: :term:`Heteromecic <heteromekes>` numbers :math:`n(n+1)` (:math:`2, 6, 12, 20, \dots`) have sides differing by :math:`1`; *promecic* rectangles :math:`a \cdot b` differ by :math:`2` or more (e.g. :math:`2 \times 4, 3 \times 6`).
2. **Same and Other (II.17.3–5)**: Unity and odd sums (:math:`n^2`) belong to the *Same*; the dyad and even sums (:math:`n(n+1)`) belong to the *Other*.
3. **Bricks, Beams, Circulars & Sphericals (II.17.6–7)**: *Bricks* (:math:`n^2 \cdot m, m < n`), *beams* (:math:`n^2 \cdot m, m > n`), *circular* squares (:math:`1, 25, 36`), and *spherical/recurrent* cubes (:math:`1, 125, 216, \dots`) ending in their base digit (:math:`1, 5, 6`).
