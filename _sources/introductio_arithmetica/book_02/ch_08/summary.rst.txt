Chapter 8: Triangular Numbers and Natural-Number Gnomons — Summary
==================================================================

.. folio:: introductio_arithmetica:0257
   :page: p. 241

Overview
--------

Nicomachus defines and constructs the first class of plane :term:`polygonal numbers <polygonos>`, the :g:`triangular numbers <polygonal-alpha-gnomons:TRI>`:

1. **Definition & Potential Unity (II.8.1–2)**: A triangular number is one whose units, arranged in a plane, form an equilateral triangle (:math:`3, 6, 10, 15, 21, 28, \dots`), with :math:`1` serving as the **potential** (*dynamei*) first triangle of side :math:`1`, and :math:`3` as the first **actual** (*energeia*) triangle of side :math:`2`.
2. **Generation by Natural-Number Gnomons (II.8.3)**: Summing consecutive terms of the natural series :math:`1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, \dots` without skipping any term builds each successive triangle by adding a new row of units underneath: :math:`T_n = 1 + 2 + \dots + n = \frac{n(n+1)}{2}`, whose side always equals the count of summed terms :math:`n`.
