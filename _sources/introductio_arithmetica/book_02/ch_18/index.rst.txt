============================================================================
Chapter 18: Pythagorean Dualities of Same (Odd/Square) & Other (Even/Oblong)
============================================================================

.. picture:: /_static/figures/heteromecic-same-other.svg
   :alt: Squares as Plato's Same (Odd Gnomons) vs. Heteromecics as Other (Even Gnomons)
   :caption: Book II, Chs. 17–19: Squares n^2 (sum of odds, Same) and Heteromecics n(n+1) (sum of evens, Other) contrasted geometrically and interwoven in sequence.

.. folio:: introductio_arithmetica:0273
   :page: p. 257

**1.** Concerning solid numbers let this much suffice for the present. Now both natural philosophers and those taking their start from mathematics declare the :term:`Same and the Other <tauton-thateron>` to be the principles of all things; and we have proved that the Same is unity together with the odd numbers shaped by it—and far more still the :g:`squares <heteromecic-same-other:SAME>` built from their cumulative sum, inasmuch as they share in equality along their sides—while the Other is the dyad and every even number shaped by it, and above all the :g:`heteromecic numbers <heteromecic-same-other:OTHER>` formed from their cumulative sum, because they share in primary inequality and otherness through the difference of their sides. We must therefore next demonstrate as an absolute necessity how in both of these, as in root principles and seeds, all the properties of number—its species, subdivisions, all its relations, polygonal figures, and the like—lie pre-contained in potentiality.

.. folio:: introductio_arithmetica:0274
   :page: p. 258

**2.** First we ought to distinguish clearly how a *promecic* number differs from a *heteromecic* one. A heteromecic, as stated above, is generated by a number multiplying the number greater than itself by one—such as 6 from twice 3, or 12 from thrice 4. A promecic, by contrast, is likewise produced by two unequal numbers, yet differing not by unity, but by some greater interval—such as twice 4, thrice 6, four times 8, and their like—having jutted forward in length, so to speak, and overstepped the difference of unity.

**3.** Since, then, squares come into being from numbers multiplied by their own length, having the same measure in length as in breadth, they may aptly be termed *self-lengthed* (*idiomēkeis*) and *same-lengthed* (*tautomēkeis*), such as twice 2, thrice 3, four times 4, and the rest. And if this holds, they are completely receptive of sameness and equality, and hence are determinate and limiting—for the equal and the same exist in one fixed, determinate mode. Conversely, because heteromecic numbers are formed not by multiplying by their own length, but by another's length extended, they are for this reason *other-lengthed* (*heteromēkeis*) and receptive of otherness, unlimitedness, and indefiniteness.

.. folio:: introductio_arithmetica:0275
   :page: p. 259

**4.** Along this line, then, all the realities of number—and all things in the cosmos fashioned after them—stand divided apart, distributed, and manifestly contrasted with one another. Rightly did the ancients, when first investigating nature, draw the primary division of cosmic ordering in this very manner: :person:`Plato <plato>` naming them the nature of the Same and that of the Other, and again the indivisible substance that ever holds fast identically and the substance that becomes divisible; while :person:`Philolaus <philolaus>` affirms that all existing things must necessarily be either unlimited, or limiting, or at once limiting and unlimited—the last of which he endorses, namely that the cosmos is fitted together out of limiting and unlimited elements together, clearly after the likeness of number. For number as a whole is likewise composed out of the monad and the dyad, the odd and the even, which manifest equality and inequality, sameness and otherness, the limiting and the unlimited, the bounded and the unbounded.
