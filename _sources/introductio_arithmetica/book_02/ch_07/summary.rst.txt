==================================================================================
Chapter 7: Point, Line, Surface, and the Triangle as Element of Polygons — Summary
==================================================================================

.. folio:: introductio_arithmetica:0255
   :page: p. 239

Overview
--------

Nicomachus maps the geometric hierarchy of point, line, surface, and solid directly onto the numerical hierarchy and proves the elementary status of the triangle:

1. **Generative Principles (II.7.1–2)**: Just as point generates line, line generates surface, and surface generates solid without being what it generates, so the :term:`monad <monas>` generates linear numbers (all integers from :math:`2` onward), linear numbers broaden into plane numbers, and plane numbers acquire depth to become solids.
2. **The Root of Plane Polygons (II.7.3)**: Plane numbers begin from **3** as their primary root and follow the natural series of sides: triangulars (:math:`3`), squares (:math:`4`), pentagonals (:math:`5`), hexagonals (:math:`6`), heptagonals (:math:`7`), and so on.
3. **Why the Triangle Is Elemental (II.7.4–5)**: Drawing lines from the center to the vertices resolves any :math:`s`-sided polygon into :math:`s` triangles, whereas applying the same dissection to a triangle resolves it only back into triangles—proving both geometrically and arithmetically that the triangle is the irreducible element of all plane figures.
