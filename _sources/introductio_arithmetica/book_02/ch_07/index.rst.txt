========================================================================
Chapter 7: Point, Line, Surface, and the Triangle as Element of Polygons
========================================================================

.. folio:: introductio_arithmetica:0255
   :page: p. 239

**1.** Thus the point is principle of interval, not interval; principle of line, not line; and line is principle of surface, not surface—principle of the two-dimensional, not two-dimensional. Naturally too, surface is principle of body, not body; principle of the three-dimensional, not three-dimensional.
**2.** In numbers likewise, the :term:`monad <monas>` is principle of every number advancing by unity along one dimension; the linear number is principle of the plane number broadening out along a second dimension; and the plane number is principle of the solid number acquiring depth as a third dimension. By subdivision, linear numbers are simply all those beginning from :math:`2` and advancing by addition of unity in one and the same

.. folio:: introductio_arithmetica:0256
   :page: p. 240

dimension; plane numbers are those starting from :math:`3` as primary root and proceeding through the successive integers, taking their names in that order—first triangles, then squares, then pentagons, then hexagons, heptagons, and so onward, named from the consecutive numbers from :math:`3`.
**3.** Thus the triangle is discovered to be the most primary and elementary figure of planes. For in rectilinear plane figures, if straight lines are drawn from the angles to the middle, every polygon resolves necessarily into as many triangles as it has sides; whereas the triangle itself, undergoing the same treatment, changes into nothing else, but into itself.

.. folio:: introductio_arithmetica:0257
   :page: p. 241

Hence among those figures also the triangle is the element: into it all the rest necessarily resolve, while it resolves into no other; from it the rest may be constructed, while it comes from none—so it is the element of the rest, and nothing is element of it. And as our discourse advances among arithmetical planes, it will confirm this same truth.
