Chapter 23: The Arithmetic Proportion & Its Difference Laws — Summary
=====================================================================

.. folio:: introductio_arithmetica:0284
   :page: p. 268

Overview
--------

Nicomachus analyzes the arithmetic :term:`proportion <analogia>`, defined by equal differences between consecutive terms (:math:`b - a = c - b = d`), and establishes four characteristic properties:

1. **Continuous & Disjunct Generation (II.23.1–4)**: Any three or more equally spaced terms in the natural series $1, 2, 3, \dots$ form an arithmetic progression—with common difference $d = 1$ if consecutive, or $d = k + 1$ when $k$ terms are skipped between neighbors. On :g:`semicircle PR <three-classical-means-semicircle:SEMICIRC>`, the arithmetic mean $A = \frac{a+c}{2}$ is realized by the :g:`radius OR <three-classical-means-semicircle:OR>` :eq:`equal to PO and OS <three-classical-means-semicircle:PO = three-classical-means-semicircle:OS>`.
2. **Sum Property (II.23.5)**: In continuous form ($a, b, c$), $2b = a + c$; in disjunct form ($a, b, c, d$ with $b - a = d - c$), $b + c = a + d$.
3. **Self-Equality of Differences & Nicomachus's Square Law (II.23.6)**: All consecutive differences stand in a $1 : 1$ ratio. More subtly, the product of the extremes falls short of the square of the mean by the square of the common difference:

   .. math::

      b^2 - ac = (b - a)(c - b) = d^2.

4. **Monotonicity of Ratios (II.23.6)**: Ratios between smaller terms exceed ratios between larger terms (e.g., in $1, 2, 3$, $2:1 > 3:2$), placing the arithmetic mean opposite the :term:`harmonic mean <harmonike>`, with the geometric mean holding equality between them.
