=====================================================================
Chapter 23: The Arithmetic Proportion & Its Difference Laws — Summary
=====================================================================

.. folio:: introductio_arithmetica:0284
   :page: p. 268

Overview
--------

Nicomachus analyzes the arithmetic :term:`proportion <analogia>`, defined by equal differences between consecutive terms (:math:`b - a = c - b = d`), and establishes four characteristic properties:

1. **Continuous & Disjunct Generation (II.23.1–4)**: Any three or more equally spaced terms in the natural series :math:`1, 2, 3, \dots` form an arithmetic progression—with common difference :math:`d = 1` if consecutive, or :math:`d = k + 1` when :math:`k` terms are skipped between neighbors. On :g:`semicircle PR <three-classical-means-semicircle:SEMICIRC>`, the arithmetic mean :math:`A = \frac{a+c}{2}` is realized by the :g:`radius OR <three-classical-means-semicircle:OR>` :eq:`equal to PO and OS <three-classical-means-semicircle:PO = three-classical-means-semicircle:OS>`.
2. **Sum Property (II.23.5)**: In continuous form (:math:`a, b, c`), :math:`2b = a + c`; in disjunct form (:math:`a, b, c, d` with :math:`b - a = d - c`), :math:`b + c = a + d`.
3. **Self-Equality of Differences & Nicomachus's Square Law (II.23.6)**: All consecutive differences stand in a :math:`1 : 1` ratio. More subtly, the product of the extremes falls short of the square of the mean by the square of the common difference:

   .. math::

      b^2 - ac = (b - a)(c - b) = d^2.

4. **Monotonicity of Ratios (II.23.6)**: Ratios between smaller terms exceed ratios between larger terms (e.g., in :math:`1, 2, 3`, :math:`2:1 > 3:2`), placing the arithmetic mean opposite the :term:`harmonic mean <harmonike>`, with the geometric mean holding equality between them.
