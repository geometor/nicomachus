Chapter 9: Square Numbers and Odd-Number Gnomons
================================================

**1.** Next after this is the :g:`square number <polygonal-alpha-gnomons:SQR>`, displaying in its diagram not three angles like the previous one, but four, yet still in equilateral arrangement—such as :math:`1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, 64, 81, 100`. Their equilateral square diagrams take shape thus:

.. code-block:: text

   α    α α    α α α    α α α α    α α α α α
        α α    α α α    α α α α    α α α α α
               α α α    α α α α    α α α α α
                        α α α α    α α α α α
                                   α α α α α

and so onward as far as you desire.

**2.** With these also it holds, just as with the preceding ones, that the advance of their sides proceeds along the natural numbers: the side

.. folio:: introductio_arithmetica:0259
   :page: p. 243

of the first potential square, :math:`1`, is :math:`1`; of the first actual square, :math:`4`, the side is :math:`2`; of the second actual, :math:`9`, the side is :math:`3`; of the third actual after it, :math:`16`, the side is :math:`4`; of the fourth, :math:`5`; of the fifth, :math:`6`; and universally so onward in order.
**3.** This number too is generated when the natural number series is set out in a row and there are accumulated upon unity not the consecutive terms—as was shown before—but all those lying one apart, namely the odd numbers. Thus :math:`1` is the first potential square; :math:`1 + 3` is the first actual square; :math:`1 + 3 + 5` is the second actual square; :math:`1 + 3 + 5 + 7` is the third actual square; the next arises when :math:`9` is piled onto the former ones, the one after it when :math:`11` is added, and so always. And in these also, the side of each contains as many units as the numbers accumulated for its generation.
