Unit IV — The Ten Neo-Pythagorean Means & the Cube's Most Perfect Proportion
============================================================================

.. rubric:: Arithmetic, Geometric & Harmonic Proportions, the Complete System of Ten Means, Philolaus's Cube, and the *Teleiotatē Analogia* (6 : 8 :: 9 : 12) (Book II, Chs. 21–29)

Book II, Chs. 21–24 — Proportion (*Analogia*) & the Arithmetic and Geometric Means
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------

*English Translation by Martin Luther D'Ooge (1926) · Hoche pp. 119–131 · D'Ooge pp. 264–273*

.. folio:: D'Ooge p. 264 · Hoche p. 119

**II.21–22 (Proportion Strictly vs. Broadly; Continuous vs. Disjunct).** After these matters, it would be the proper time to introduce the nature of **proportions** (:term:`analogiai <analogia>`), a subject most essential for speculation on the nature of the universe and for the doctrines of music, astronomy, geometry, and perusal of the works of the ancients. A :term:`proportion <analogia>` is, strictly speaking, the bringing together to the same point of two or more ratios; more broadly, of two or more relations even if they are subjected not to the same ratio, but to a regulated difference or some similar law. The **first three proportions**, acknowledged by all the ancients—:person:`Pythagoras <pythagoras>`, :person:`Plato <plato>`, and :person:`Aristotle <aristotle>`—are the **Arithmetic**, **Geometric**, and :term:`Harmonic <harmonike>` (originally called :term:`subcontrary <hypenantia>`, *hypenantia*); three more exist directly opposite to them (Means 4, 5, 6), and four others discovered by later investigators (:person:`Myonides <myonides>` and Euphranor) bring the total to **ten**—the Pythagorean *Decad*, most perfect of numbers! A proportion in **three terms** (:math:`a : b :: b : c`, middle term taken twice) is **continuous** (*synēgmenē*), whereas one in **four distinct terms** (:math:`a : b :: c : d`, e.g. :math:`6 : 8 :: 9 : 12` in arithmetic difference or geometric/harmonic ratio) is **disjunct** (*diezeugmenē*).

.. folio:: D'Ooge p. 267 · Hoche p. 123

**II.23 (The Arithmetic Proportion & Its Subtle Square Property).** We have an **arithmetic proportion** whenever three or more terms exceed and are exceeded by **equal differences** (:math:`c - b = b - a = d`), as :math:`1, 2, 3` or :math:`6, 9, 12` (where :math:`b = \frac{a + c}{2}`, geometrically :g:`the radius OR = A <three-classical-means-semicircle:OR>` :eq:`equal to PO and OS <three-classical-means-semicircle:PO = three-classical-means-semicircle:OS>` on :g:`semicircle PR <three-classical-means-semicircle:SEMICIRC>`). Notice here two remarkable properties:

1. While the differences are equal, the **ratios are larger among the smaller terms** and **smaller among the larger terms** (in :math:`1, 2, 3`, :math:`2:1 > 3:2`; in :math:`6, 9, 12`, :math:`9:6 = 3:2 > 12:9 = 4:3`).
2. A subtle property missed by most: **the product of the extremes falls short of the square of the middle term by the exact square of the common difference** (:math:`b^2 - ac = d^2`)! Thus in :math:`6, 9, 12` (difference :math:`d = 3`), :math:`6 \times 12 = 72`, which plus :math:`3^2 = 9` equals :math:`9^2 = 81`!

.. folio:: D'Ooge p. 269 · Hoche p. 126

**II.24 (The Geometric Proportion — The Only Proportion Strictly So Called).** The **geometric proportion**, the only one strictly entitled to the name *analogia*, occurs when the terms stand to one another in **the same ratio** (:math:`c : b = b : a`), as :math:`1, 2, 4` or :math:`4, 6, 9`, so that the product of the extremes **equals the exact square of the mean** (:math:`ac = b^2`, realized geometrically by :g:`the perpendicular ordinate QS = G <three-classical-means-semicircle:QS>` to :g:`semicircle PR <three-classical-means-semicircle:SEMICIRC>`). Moreover, in continuous geometric progressions, consecutive differences retain the exact same ratio as the terms themselves, plane numbers are bonded by **one** geometric mean (e.g. :math:`4, 6, 9`), and solid cubes are bonded by **two** geometric means in continuous proportion (e.g. :math:`8, 12, 18, 27` between :math:`2^3` and :math:`3^3`, cf. :cite:`plato:timaeus:27d-36d`, 31c–32b; :cite:`euclid:elements:vii-ix`, VIII.11–12).

Book II, Chs. 25–27 — The Harmonic Mean, Philolaus's Cube & Musical Consonances
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

*Hoche pp. 131–139 · D'Ooge pp. 273–281*

.. folio:: D'Ooge p. 273 · Hoche p. 131

**II.25 (The Harmonic Proportion).** The third proportion, the :term:`harmonic <harmonike>` (*harmonikē*), occurs whenever among three terms the greatest stands to the smallest **in the same ratio as the difference between greatest and middle stands to the difference between middle and smallest**:

.. math::

   (c - b) : (b - a) = c : a \iff b = \frac{2ac}{a + c},

as in :math:`3, 4, 6` (where :math:`6 : 3 = (6 - 4) : (4 - 3) = 2 : 1`) or :math:`6, 8, 12` (where :math:`12 : 6 = (12 - 8) : (8 - 6) = 2 : 1`, geometrically realized on :g:`semicircle PR <three-classical-means-semicircle:SEMICIRC>` by :g:`radial projection segment QF = H <three-classical-means-semicircle:SF>` satisfying :math:`G^2 = A \cdot H`). Contrary to the arithmetic mean, in the harmonic proportion **the ratios among the larger terms are greater** than those among the smaller terms (in :math:`3, 4, 6`, :math:`6:4 = 3:2 > 4:3`; in :math:`6, 8, 12`, :math:`12:8 = 3:2 > 8:6 = 4:3`—which is why it was originally called :term:`subcontrary <hypenantia>`!), and adding the extremes and multiplying by the mean gives **twice the product of the extremes** (:math:`(a + c)b = 2ac`).

.. folio:: D'Ooge p. 277 · Hoche p. 134

**II.26 (Musical Consonances & Philolaus's Cube as "Geometric Harmony").** Why was this proportion named **harmonic**? First, because in :math:`6, 8, 12` its extreme ratio :math:`12 : 6 = 2 : 1` is the fundamental consonance **diapason** (octave), :math:`12 : 8 = 3 : 2` is the **diapente** (fifth), and :math:`8 : 6 = 4 : 3` is the **diatessaron** (fourth)! Second, and most strikingly, according to :person:`Philolaus <philolaus>`, because of its presence in all **geometric harmony**—which Philolaus identifies with the **Cube**, harmonious across all three dimensions (*platē*, *mēkē*, *bathē*): for every **cube** has **6 plane faces**, **8 solid angles (vertices)**, and **12 bounding edges**, forming the exact harmonic triple **6, 8, 12**!

**II.27 (Rules for Inserting the Three Classical Means).** Between any two extremes (both even or both odd, like :math:`10` and :math:`40`), the **arithmetic** mean is :math:`\frac{a + c}{2} = 25`, the **geometric** mean is :math:`\sqrt{ac} = \sqrt{400} = 20`, and the **harmonic** mean is :math:`\frac{2ac}{a + c} = \frac{800}{50} = 16` (:math:`16 < 20 < 25` with :math:`20^2 = 16 \times 25 = 400`).

Book II, Ch. 28 — The Seven Additional Means Completing the Ten Proportions
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

*Hoche pp. 139–144 · D'Ooge pp. 281–284*

.. folio:: D'Ooge p. 281 · Hoche p. 139

**II.28.1–11 (The Complete Decad of Proportions).** Following the three classical means (:g:`Means 1–3 <three-classical-means-semicircle:TENMEANS>`), three subcontrary means arise by reversing their defining ratios, and four more discovered by later Pythagoreans (:person:`Myonides <myonides>` and Euphranor) compare the difference of extremes :math:`(c - a)` against one of the inner differences:

* **4th Mean (Subcontrary to Harmonic)**: :math:`(c - b) : (b - a) = a : c`, exemplified in :math:`3, 5, 6` (:math:`(6-5):(5-3) = 1:2 = 3:6`).
* **5th Mean (Subcontrary to Geometric I)**: :math:`(c - b) : (b - a) = a : b`, exemplified in :math:`2, 4, 5` (:math:`1:2 = 2:4`).
* **6th Mean (Subcontrary to Geometric II)**: :math:`(c - b) : (b - a) = b : c`, exemplified in :math:`1, 4, 6` (:math:`2:3 = 4:6`).
* **7th Mean**: :math:`(c - a) : (b - a) = c : a`, exemplified in :math:`6, 8, 9` (:math:`(9-6):(8-6) = 3:2 = 9:6`).
* **8th Mean**: :math:`(c - a) : (c - b) = c : a`, exemplified in :math:`6, 7, 9` (:math:`(9-6):(9-7) = 3:2 = 9:6`).
* **9th Mean**: :math:`(c - a) : (b - a) = b : a`, exemplified correctly in :math:`4, 6, 7` (:math:`(7-4):(6-4) = 3:2 = 6:4`; see Critical Notes on the manuscript variant :math:`4, 7, 8`).
* **10th Mean (Additive / Fibonacci Identity)**: :math:`(c - a) : (c - b) = b : a`, which simplifies identically for :math:`b \neq c` to :math:`c - a = b \iff a + b = c`, exemplified in :g:`3, 5, 8 <three-classical-means-semicircle:MEAN10>` (:math:`(8-3):(8-5) = 5:3` and :math:`3 + 5 = 8`)!

Book II, Ch. 29 — The Most Perfect Proportion (*Teleiotatē Analogia*, 6 : 8 :: 9 : 12)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

*Hoche pp. 144–147 · D'Ooge pp. 284–286*

.. picture:: /_static/figures/cube-most-perfect-proportion.svg
   :alt: The Cube and Most Perfect Proportion 6 : 8 :: 9 : 12
   :caption: Book II, Ch. 29: The Cube (6 faces, 8 vertices, 12 edges) and Monochord (6 : 8 :: 9 : 12) uniting arithmetic (9), harmonic (8), and geometric (6*12 = 8*9 = 72) proportion with all Pythagorean musical consonances.

.. folio:: D'Ooge p. 284 · Hoche p. 144

**II.29.1–5 (Crown of the Treatise).** Let us crown our *Introduction* with the :term:`Most Perfect Proportion <teleiotate-analogia>` (*teleiotatē analogia*)—three-dimensional, four-termed, containing within a single tetrad **all three classical proportions** together with **every musical consonance** and the **three-dimensional solid architecture of the Cube**:

Let there be taken two solid cubes—for example the even cube :math:`8 = 2 \times 2 \times 2` and the odd cube :math:`27 = 3 \times 3 \times 3` (or most simply in root integers, the extreme terms **6** and **12**, which themselves arise from the Cube's :g:`6 plane faces <cube-most-perfect-proportion:FACES6>`, :g:`8 solid vertices <cube-most-perfect-proportion:VERTS8>`, and :g:`12 linear edges <cube-most-perfect-proportion:EDGES12>`). Between **6** and **12** insert **both** interior means on :g:`the single four-term monochord 6 : 8 :: 9 : 12 <cube-most-perfect-proportion:MONOCHORD>`:

1. **Geometric Proportion across all four terms**: :math:`12 : 9 = 8 : 6` (:math:`4 : 3`) and alternately :math:`12 : 8 = 9 : 6` (:math:`3 : 2`), while the product of the extremes equals the product of the means: :math:`6 \times 12 = 8 \times 9 = \mathbf{72}`!
2. **Arithmetic Proportion**: **9** exceeds **6** by the exact same difference (:math:`3`) by which **12** exceeds **9** (:math:`6, 9, 12`), and equals half the sum of the extremes (:math:`\frac{6 + 12}{2} = 9`).
3. **Harmonic Proportion**: **8** exceeds **6** and is exceeded by **12** by the exact same fraction (**one-third**) of each extreme (:math:`8 - 6 = 2 = \frac{1}{3} \cdot 6` and :math:`12 - 8 = 4 = \frac{1}{3} \cdot 12`), matching the Cube's **6** faces, **8** vertices, and **12** edges!
4. **Complete Pythagorean Musical Scale**:
   - Extreme to extreme :math:`12 : 6 = 2 : 1` sounds the :g:`Diapason (Octave) <cube-most-perfect-proportion:OCTAVE>`.
   - Greater extreme to harmonic mean (:math:`12 : 8`) and arithmetic mean to lesser extreme (:math:`9 : 6`) both equal :math:`3 : 2`, sounding the :g:`Diapente (Fifth) <cube-most-perfect-proportion:FIFTH>`.
   - Greater extreme to arithmetic mean (:math:`12 : 9`) and harmonic mean to lesser extreme (:math:`8 : 6`) both equal :math:`4 : 3`, sounding the :g:`Diatessaron (Fourth) <cube-most-perfect-proportion:FOURTH>`.
   - Arithmetic mean to harmonic mean (:math:`9 : 8`) sounds the :g:`Epogdoos Whole Tone <cube-most-perfect-proportion:TONE>`, the common measure of musical intervals!
