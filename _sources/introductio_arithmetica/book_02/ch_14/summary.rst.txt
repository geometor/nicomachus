======================================================================
Chapter 14: Truncated, Bi-Truncated & Tri-Truncated Pyramids — Summary
======================================================================

.. folio:: introductio_arithmetica:0266
   :page: p. 250

Overview
--------

Nicomachus extends pyramidal generation to square and higher polygonal bases and classifies frusta by how many summit layers are omitted:

1. **Square-Based Pyramids (II.14.1)**: Summing consecutive squares :math:`1, 4, 9, 16, \dots` generates the square pyramidal series :math:`1, 5, 14, 30, \dots`, where each edge equals the count of stacked layers.
2. **Primacy of the Triangle (II.14.2)**: Every polygonal pyramid has triangular lateral faces converging to the apex, confirming the triangle as the elementary plane form.
3. **Truncated Pyramids (II.14.3)**: Omitting the apex unit :math:`1` produces a *truncated* (*kolouros*) pyramid; omitting :math:`1` and the second layer yields a *bi-truncated* (*dikolouros*); omitting three top layers gives a *tri-truncated* (*trikolouros*), and so on.
