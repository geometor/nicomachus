Chapter 3: The Four Sister Sciences (Mathemata / Quadrivium) — Notes
====================================================================

.. folio:: introductio_arithmetica:0200
   :page: p. 184

Archival Witnesses & Apparatus
------------------------------

* **English Witness**: D'Ooge (1926), pp. 184–187 (folios ``introductio_arithmetica:0200``–``0203``).
* **Greek Witness**: Hoche (1866), pp. 5–9 (folios ``hoche_1866:0023``–``0027``).
* **Boethius Parallel**: :person:`Boethius <boethius>`, *De Institutione Arithmetica* I.1 (:cite:`boethius:friedlein:1867`, pp. 7–10 Friedlein), where Nicomachus's "four methods" (*tessares methodoi*), "bridges," and "ladders" are rendered by the Latin coinage *quadruvium* ("fourfold crossroads").

Philosophical & Philological Notes
----------------------------------

* **Archytas Fragment DK 47 B 1 (I.3.4)**: Nicomachus preserves the shorter excerpt of :person:`Archytas <archytas>`'s *Harmonics* opening (compare Porphyry *In Ptol. Harm.* p. 236 and Iamblichus *In Nicom.* p. 9 Pistelli). Note the Doric dialect forms (*dokounti emmenai adelphea*, *tas ton holon physios*, *ammin*).
* **Epinomis 991d–992a (I.3.5)**: Nicomachus cites the *Epinomis* as "the thirteenth book of the *Laws*, which some entitle *The Philosopher*," quoting from memory with slight textual variation from the Platonic vulgate (D'Ooge p. 185 n. 3).
* **Proclus Reception**: :person:`Proclus <proclus>` reproduces Nicomachus's exact fourfold taxonomy of quantity and magnitude in his Prologue to Euclid (:cite:`proclus:in-euclid:35-37`, pp. 35.17–36.25 Friedlein).
