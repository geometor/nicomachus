======================================================================
Chapter 3: The Four Sister Sciences (Mathemata / Quadrivium) — Summary
======================================================================

.. folio:: introductio_arithmetica:0200
   :page: p. 184

Overview
--------

Having delimited finite discrete :term:`quantity <poson>` (*to poson*) and continuous :term:`size <pelikon>` (*to pēlikon*), Nicomachus bifurcates each into two branches to establish the canonical four :term:`mathematical sciences <mathemata>` (*mathemata*):

1. **Quantity (*to poson*)**:
   * **Absolute quantity** (*kath' heauto*, such as even, odd, perfect): studied by :g:`Arithmetic <quadrivium-tetrad:ARITH>`.
   * **Relative quantity** (*pros allo*, such as double, half, *hemiolios*, *epitritos*): studied by :g:`Music <quadrivium-tetrad:MUSIC>`.
2. **Size (*to pēlikon*)**:
   * **Stationary magnitude** (*en monēi kai stasei*): studied by :g:`Geometry <quadrivium-tetrad:GEOM>`.
   * **Moving and revolving magnitude** (*en kinēsei kai periphorāi*): studied by :g:`Astronomy <quadrivium-tetrad:ASTRON>` (*sphairikē*).
3. **Ancient Authorities & Pedagogical Ascent (I.3.3–7)**: Nicomachus cites :person:`Androcydes <androcydes>`, :person:`Archytas <archytas>` (*Harmonics* DK 47 B 1, calling the four sciences "sisters"), and :person:`Plato <plato>` (*Epinomis* 991d–992a and *Republic* VII 527d–e). The four sciences act as "ladders and bridges" (*klimakes kai gephyrai*) carrying the mind from sensible flux to eternal immaterial truth and rekindling the "eye of the soul."
