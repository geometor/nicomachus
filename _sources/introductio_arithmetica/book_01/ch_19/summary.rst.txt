=======================================================================
Chapter 19: Second Species of Inequality: The Superparticular — Summary
=======================================================================

.. folio:: introductio_arithmetica:0231
   :page: p. 215

Overview
--------

Nicomachus develops the :g:`superparticular <five-species-ratios:SP2>` (:term:`epimorios <epimorios>`, :math:`(n+1):n`) and demonstrates its natural derivation from the multiples via a :math:`10 \times 10` multiplication table:

1. **Definitions & First Two Subspecies (I.19.1–7)**: A superparticular contains the whole of the lesser number plus one aliquot part of it. When that part is a half, the ratio is :term:`sesquialter <hemiolios>` (:math:`3:2, 6:4, 9:6, 12:8, \dots`); when a third, :term:`sesquitertian <epitritos>` (:math:`4:3, 8:6, 12:9, \dots`). The root terms (*pythmenes*) are consecutive integers, while the :math:`k`-th pair has :math:`k - 1` integers between them.
2. **The 10 × 10 Table & Priority of Multiples (I.19.8–16)**: Comparing rows/columns arranged in gnomonic gamma-shapes (:math:`\Gamma`) and chi-crosses (:math:`\mathrm{X}`), rows :math:`2, 3, 4, \dots` compared with row :math:`1` give multiples, whereas adjacent rows (:math:`3` vs. :math:`2`, :math:`4` vs. :math:`3`, etc.) give superparticulars.
3. **Square–Heteromecic Diagonal Identity (I.19.17–19)**: The main diagonal holds the squares :math:`n^2` (:math:`1, 4, 9, \dots, 100`), flanked by heteromecic (oblong) products :math:`n(n+1)`, exhibiting the Pythagorean identity :math:`n^2 + (n+1)^2 + 2n(n+1) = (2n+1)^2` and :math:`(n-1)n + n(n+1) + 2n^2 = (2n)^2`.
