===========================================
Chapter 14: Superabundant Numbers — Summary
===========================================

.. folio:: introductio_arithmetica:0223
   :page: p. 207

Overview
--------

Returning to the classification of simple even numbers, Nicomachus divides them into two opposed extremes of inequality—**superabundant** (*hyperteleis*) and **deficient** (*ellipeis*)—with :term:`perfect <teleios>` (*teleioi*) numbers standing at the equal mean between them:

1. **Moral and Cosmic Analogy of Excess, Defect, and Mean (I.14.1–2)**: All vice, disease, disproportion, and deformity belong to excess or deficiency, while virtues, moderation, health, and beauty reside in the equal mean.
2. **Definition and Examples of Superabundant Numbers (I.14.3–4)**: A number is *superabundant* when the sum of its proper aliquot parts exceeds the number itself—like an animal born with superfluous limbs. Thus :math:`12` has proper parts :math:`6 + 4 + 3 + 2 + 1 = 16 > 12`, and :math:`24` has proper parts :math:`12 + 8 + 6 + 4 + 3 + 2 + 1 = 36 > 24`.
