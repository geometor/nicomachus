=================================================================================
Chapter 23: The Multiple Superpartient & Unfolding All Inequalities from Equality
=================================================================================

.. picture:: /_static/figures/equality-unfolding-tree.svg
   :alt: The Three Prostagma Commands Unfolding All Ratios from Equality
   :caption: Book I, Ch. 23: Starting from equality (a, a, a), applying (a, a+b, a+2b+c) and sibling reversal unfolds every species of inequality.

.. folio:: introductio_arithmetica:0240
   :page: p. 224

**1.** The :g:`multiple superpartient <five-species-ratios:SP5>` is the remaining relation of number;
this relation, along with its counterpart named with the prefix *sub-*, occurs whenever a number contains the compared number as a whole more than once—twice, three times, or any number of times—and in addition several parts of it more than one, whether 2, 3, 4, 5, and so on. Because of what we explained earlier, these parts are not halves, but thirds, fourths, fifths, and according to the same sequence.

**2.** From what has already been stated, it is easy to grasp the species of this relation as well, since it varies in exactly the same manner as the one before it: double superbipartient, then double supertripartient, then double superquadripartient, and analogously—for instance, 8 to 3 is double superbipartient, and 16 to 6 is double superbipartient, and in general the numbers from 8 onward differing by 8 compared to those from 3 differing by 3, rank for rank;
and by following what has been said above, one could discover the orderly arrangement of the remaining species as well; here too, one must understand the reciprocal naming of the compared lesser term as advancing and shifting alongside with the prefix *sub-*.

**3.** Thus the ten arithmetical relations reach the end of their treatment for us, as in a first introduction. Yet there is a subtler method,

.. folio:: introductio_arithmetica:0241
   :page: p. 225

and one most necessary for the entire natural philosophy of the universe,
which sets before us most plainly and beyond dispute that, first, what is fair, bounded, and accessible to knowledge is by nature prior to the unbounded, incomprehensible, and ugly; and second, that even the parts and species of the infinite

.. folio:: introductio_arithmetica:0242
   :page: p. 226

and unbounded are shaped and bounded by the former, attaining their fitting order and symmetry, and as though beneath a seal or measure all things falling under it partake of likeness and common name.

**4.** For thus with good reason will the rational part of the soul bring order to the irrational, and spirit and appetite—arrayed within the two species of inequality—be harmonized by the reasoning faculty as by a kind of :term:`equality <isotes>` and sameness; and from this equalization will rightly issue for us the so-called ethical virtues: temperance, courage, gentleness, self-mastery, endurance, and the like.

**5.** Come then, let us examine what kind of theorem this is that leads into these natural truths. It demonstrates that out of sole and primal equality, as from a mother and root, all the manifold species and varieties of inequality are born. Let there be set before us in three terms any equal numbers: first :g:`units <equality-unfolding-tree:ROOT111>`, then twos in another set of three, then threes, then fours, then fives, and so on as far as you wish.

**6.** When these are set forth in this manner, not by human contrivance but by a divine reason and by nature herself, first will be generated the multiples—and of these, the double will lead, after it the triple, upon that the quadruple, then the quintuple, and according to the order we learned earlier to infinity; second, the superparticular—and of this again the foremost species, the sesquialter, will lead, upon it the next, the sesquitertian,
upon these the next in rank, the sesquiquartan, sesquiquintan, sesquisextan, and analogously to infinity; third, the superpartient—and again of this itself the superbipartient will lead, directly following it the supertripartient, then the

.. folio:: introductio_arithmetica:0243
   :page: p. 227

superquadripartient, and immediately the superquintipartient, and as far as one advances in agreement with what went before.

**7.** We must then possess certain commands, like unswerving and inviolable natural laws, by which all the foregoing progression and advance from equality may proceed smoothly without breaking rank. And the commands are these: make the first equal to the first; the second equal to the first together with the second; and the third equal to the first, twice the second, and the third together.

**8.** For as you construct with these laws, straightway all the species of the multiple will sprout and grow in order from the three exposed terms of equality, without your contriving or assisting in anything: out of equality straightway :g:`the double <equality-unfolding-tree:N124>`, out of the double straightway :g:`the triple <equality-unfolding-tree:N139>`, out of the triple next :g:`the quadruple <equality-unfolding-tree:N1416>`, and out of this the quintuple in due order, and so forever.

**9.** And out of these very orderly multiples, when :g:`reversed <equality-unfolding-tree:REDUCE>`, straightway are generated by a natural necessity through the same three commands the superparticulars—and these not at random or in disorder, but in their proper sequence: from the first, the double, reversed comes the first, :g:`the sesquialter <equality-unfolding-tree:N469>`; from the second, the triple, comes the second among them, :g:`the sesquitertian <equality-unfolding-tree:N91216>`; then the sesquiquartan from the quadruple; and simply each from the one after which it is named.

**10.** From a fresh start, when the superparticulars themselves are set out just as they sprang up, yet in reverse order, the superpartients that follow them in nature are generated: from the sesquialter comes the superbipartient, from the sesquitertian the supertripartient, the superquadripartient from the sesquiquartan, and

.. folio:: introductio_arithmetica:0244
   :page: p. 228

so on to infinity in the same proportion.

**11.** Yet when they are not reversed, but the orderly superparticulars are set down in direct order, the multiple superparticulars are generated through the same commands: the double sesquialter from the first, the sesquialter; the double sesquitertian from the second, the sesquitertian; the double sesquiquartan from the third, the sesquiquartan; and always so.

**12.** Out of those generated by reversal of the superparticulars—namely, the superpartients—and out of those not by reversal—namely, the multiple superparticulars—again in the same manner through the same commands, when placed both directly and in reverse, are generated the numbers displaying the remaining relations.

**13.** For all that has been said—their generation, order, directness, and reversal—let the following examples suffice us as a reminder.

**14.** From the sesquialter relation and proportion, when reversed from the greater term, is constituted a relation in superpartient ratios, the superbipartient; and when set directly from the lesser term, a multiple superparticular, namely the double sesquialter—as from :g:`9, 6, 4 <equality-unfolding-tree:N469>`, either :g:`9, 15, 25 <equality-unfolding-tree:N91525>` or :g:`4, 10, 25 <equality-unfolding-tree:N41025>`.
From the sesquitertian relation, from the greater term comes a superpartient, namely the supertripartient, and from the lesser the double sesquitertian—as from :g:`16, 12, 9 <equality-unfolding-tree:N91216>`, either 16, 28, 49 or 9, 21, 49. And from the sesquiquartan relation, from the exceeding term comes a superpartient, namely the superquadripartient, and from the lesser a multiple superparticular, namely the double sesquiquartan—as from 25, 20, 16, either 25, 45, 81 or 16, 36, 81.

**15.** Across all these separated relations and

.. folio:: introductio_arithmetica:0245
   :page: p. 229

the one from which both spring, the last square remains the same while the first passes into the lesser, and in every case the extremes are squares.

**16.** Furthermore, in yet another way out of the superpartients emerge the multiple superpartients and superpartients of another kind: for instance, from the superbipartient, from the lesser term comes the double superbipartient, and from the greater the super-3/5-partient (*trisepipemptos*), as from :g:`9, 15, 25 <equality-unfolding-tree:N91525>`, either :g:`9, 24, 64 <equality-unfolding-tree:N92464>` or 25, 40, 64;
from the supertripartient, from the smaller term comes the double supertripartient, and from the greater the super-4/7-partient (*tetrakisephebdomos*), as in 16, 28, 49, either 16, 44, 121 or 49, 77, 121.

**17.** Again, from the superquadripartient, such as 25, 45, 81, from the lesser comes the double superquadripartient in 25, 70, 196, and from the greater once more a superpartient, the super-5/9-partient (*pentakisepenatos*), as in 81, 126, 196; and in the succeeding cases you will find them proportional and well-fitted to infinity.
