===========================================================================================
Chapter 23: The Multiple Superpartient & Unfolding All Inequalities from Equality — Summary
===========================================================================================

.. folio:: introductio_arithmetica:0240
   :page: p. 224

Overview
--------

Nicomachus completes the classification of the ten arithmetic relations with the :g:`multiple superpartient <five-species-ratios:SP5>` (*pollaplasiepimerēs*), and then crowns Book I with the generative theorem deriving **all species of inequality from Equality**:

1. **Multiple Superpartient (I.23.1–3)**: The greater term contains the lesser more than once *plus* more than one aliquot part of the lesser (:math:`n + \frac{p}{q}` with :math:`n \ge 2, 1 < p < q`), yielding double superbipartient (:math:`8:3`), double supertripartient (:math:`11:4`), triple superbipartient (:math:`11:3`), and their ``sub-`` reciprocals.
2. **Cosmological Significance of Equality (I.23.4–5)**: Just as determinate form bounds the indeterminate in nature and reason orders passion and appetite into ethical virtues, so primordial :term:`equality <isotes>` is the mother and root from which every species of inequality unfolds.
3. **The Three *Prostagma* Rules (I.23.6–17)**: Starting from any three equal terms :math:`(a, a, a)`—such as :g:`(1, 1, 1) <equality-unfolding-tree:ROOT111>`—and forming the new triple :math:`(a,\; a+b,\; a+2b+c)` yields:

   * First, all **multiples** in order: doubles :g:`(1, 2, 4) <equality-unfolding-tree:N124>`, triples :g:`(1, 3, 9) <equality-unfolding-tree:N139>`, quadruples :g:`(1, 4, 16) <equality-unfolding-tree:N1416>`, etc.
   * Second, reversing the multiples (*anastrophe*) and applying the same three rules generates all **superparticulars**: :g:`(4, 6, 9) <equality-unfolding-tree:N469>` sesquialters, :g:`(9, 12, 16) <equality-unfolding-tree:N91216>` sesquitertians, etc.
   * Third, keeping superparticulars in direct order yields **multiple superparticulars** (:g:`(4, 10, 25) <equality-unfolding-tree:N41025>`), while reversing superparticulars yields **superpartients** (:g:`(9, 15, 25) <equality-unfolding-tree:N91525>`), and applying the rules to superpartients in direct and reversed order generates **multiple superpartients** (:g:`(9, 24, 64) <equality-unfolding-tree:N92464>`) and higher superpartients—every triple having square extremes and a mean equal to the product of their sides.
