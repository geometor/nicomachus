===============================================
Chapter 10: The Odd-Times Even Number — Summary
===============================================

.. folio:: introductio_arithmetica:0214
   :page: p. 198

Overview
--------

Completing the tripartition of **even** numbers, Nicomachus characterizes the **odd-times even** (*perissartios*, numbers of the form :math:`2^k(2m + 1)` with :math:`k \ge 2, m \ge 1`) as a true mean (*mesotēs*) and natural blend (*physikon migma*) uniting the two extremes:

1. **Multiple Halvings Terminating Short of Unity (I.10.2–4)**: Every odd-times even number (:math:`12, 24, 28, 40, \dots`) can be halved at least twice, yet repeated bisection always stops at an odd number greater than :math:`1`. Consequently it possesses multiple even-divisible upper parts and multiple odd-indivisible lower parts.
2. **Mixed Parity of Name and Value (I.10.5)**: Some proper parts match in parity between name and value (e.g., in :math:`24`: :math:`\frac{1}{2} \to 12`, :math:`\frac{1}{4} \to 6`, :math:`\frac{1}{6} \to 4`, :math:`\frac{1}{12} \to 2`), while others invert parity (e.g., :math:`\frac{1}{3} \to 8`, :math:`\frac{1}{8} \to 3`, :math:`\frac{1}{24} \to 1`).
3. **Cartesian Multiplication Table (I.10.6–10)**: Multiplying the odd sequence :math:`(2m + 1) \in \{3, 5, 7, 9, \dots\}` by the even-times even sequence :math:`2^k \in \{4, 8, 16, 32, \dots\}` produces a 2D rectangular array whose **columns** (*platos*, breadth) follow arithmetic progressions with common difference :math:`2^{k+1}` (preserving the arithmetic mean law of Ch. 9) and whose **rows** (*mēkos*, length) follow geometric progressions of ratio :math:`2` (preserving the geometric mean law of Ch. 8).
