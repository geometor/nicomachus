=====================================================
Chapter 12: Secondary and Composite Numbers — Summary
=====================================================

.. folio:: introductio_arithmetica:0218
   :page: p. 202

Overview
--------

Nicomachus defines the second, opposite extreme among the odd numbers—the **secondary and composite** (*deuteros kai synthetos*) species:

1. **Origin by Multiplication (I.12.1)**: A secondary and composite number is odd, yet lacks elementary primacy because it arises by combining prior numbers. Consequently, beside the part sharing its own name (unity), it possesses at least one part with a different denominator, equal to the prior odd factors from which it is composed (e.g., :math:`9, 15, 21, 25, 27, 33, 35, 39`).
2. **Dual Nomenclature (I.12.2)**: It is called *secondary* because another number measures it prior to its own self-measurement, and *composite* because it can be decomposed into the equal groups corresponding to the numbers whose multiplication produced it, whereas no such decomposition into equal parts larger than unity exists for a prime.
