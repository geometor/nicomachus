===============================================================
Chapter 18: First Species of Inequality: The Multiple — Summary
===============================================================

.. folio:: introductio_arithmetica:0230
   :page: p. 214

Overview
--------

Nicomachus examines the first and most fundamental species of inequality—the :g:`multiple <five-species-ratios:SP1>` (:term:`pollaplasios <pollaplasios>`) and its correlative the **submultiple** (*hypopollaplasios*):

1. **Definitions (I.18.1–3)**: A number is *multiple* when it contains the compared number as a whole more than once (:math:`m \cdot b : b` for :math:`m \ge 2`: double, triple, quadruple, etc.); conversely, the *submultiple* measures the larger number completely more than once (subdouble, subtriple, subquadruple, etc.).
2. **Natural Progression & Parity Laws (I.18.4–7)**: Comparing the natural sequence :math:`1, 2, 3, 4, \dots` with regular strides yields every species of multiplicity:
   - **Doubles** (:math:`2, 4, 6, 8, \dots`): every even number, skipping 1.
   - **Triples** (:math:`3, 6, 9, 12, \dots`): every 3rd number (skipping 2), alternating odd and even.
   - **Quadruples** (:math:`4, 8, 12, 16, \dots`): every 4th number (skipping 3), all even (every alternate even number); likewise sextuples skip 2 evens, octuples skip 3 evens.
   - **Quintuples** (:math:`5, 10, 15, \dots`): every 5th number (skipping 4), alternating odd and even.
