==============================================
Chapter 9: The Even-Times Odd Number — Summary
==============================================

.. folio:: introductio_arithmetica:0212
   :page: p. 196

Overview
--------

Turning to the opposite extreme of the even numbers, Nicomachus defines and characterizes the **even-times odd** (*artioperittos*, numbers of the form :math:`2(2k + 1) = 4k + 2` for :math:`k \ge 1`):

1. **Single Bisection to Odd Halves (I.9.1)**: An even-times odd number splits into two equal parts once, immediately yielding two indivisible odd halves (:math:`6, 10, 14, 18, 22, 26, \dots`).
2. **Parity Inversion Between Name and Value (I.9.2)**: Every proper aliquot part exhibits opposite parity between its denominator (*onoma*) and its quotient (*dynamis*): e.g., in :math:`18`, the :math:`\frac{1}{2}`-part (even name) is :math:`9` (odd value), whereas the :math:`\frac{1}{3}`-part (odd name) is :math:`6` (even value).
3. **Arithmetic Progression & Natural Spacing (I.9.3–5)**: Doubling the odd sequence :math:`3, 5, 7, 9, \dots` produces the arithmetic progression :math:`6, 10, 14, 18, \dots` with common difference :math:`4` (:math:`2 \times 2 = 4`), spaced every fifth position in the natural numbers (skipping three terms).
4. **Additive vs. Multiplicative Symmetry (I.9.6)**: Whereas divisors of :math:`2^k` satisfy a geometric mean relation (:math:`a_0 a_k = m^2`), the ordered progression of even-times odd numbers satisfies an arithmetic mean relation (:math:`a_0 + a_k = 2m` or :math:`m_1 + m_2`).
