======================================================================
Chapter 1: Philosophy as Love of Wisdom & Immaterial Reality — Summary
======================================================================

.. folio:: introductio_arithmetica:0197
   :page: p. 181

Overview
--------

Nicomachus opens the *Introduction to Arithmetic* by grounding mathematics in the Pythagorean definition of :term:`philosophy <philosophia>` (*philosophia*) as the desire for and pursuit of wisdom (*sophia*).

1. **Restriction of "Wisdom" (I.1.1)**: Prior to :person:`Pythagoras <pythagoras>`, any skilled artisan (carpenter, cobbler, helmsman) was called *sophos*. Pythagoras restricted *sophia* to the steadfast scientific comprehension of unchanging reality.
2. **Immaterial Realities vs. Bodily Flux (I.1.2–4)**: Material bodies are subject to perpetual flow and alteration, imitating primordial changeable matter. By contrast, incorporeal forms conceived alongside matter—qualities, quantities, configurations, :term:`equality <isotes>`, relations, places, times—are immovable and immutable in themselves, changing only accidentally through their participation in bodies. Wisdom studies these immaterial realities primarily, and bodies only derivatively.
