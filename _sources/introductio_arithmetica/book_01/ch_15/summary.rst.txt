=======================================
Chapter 15: Deficient Numbers — Summary
=======================================

.. folio:: introductio_arithmetica:0224
   :page: p. 208

Overview
--------

Opposite to the superabundant numbers stand **deficient** (*ellipeis*) numbers, whose proper aliquot parts sum to less than the whole:

1. **Biological Analogy (I.15.1)**: Nicomachus compares a deficient number to an animal missing natural limbs—a one-eyed Cyclops (quoting Hesiod, *Theogony* 145), a one-handed man, or one lacking fingers or a tongue.
2. **Numerical Examples (I.15.1–2)**: The number :math:`8` has proper parts :math:`4 + 2 + 1 = 7 < 8`, and :math:`14` has proper parts :math:`7 + 2 + 1 = 10 < 14`.
