===============================================
Chapter 8: The Even-Times Even Number — Summary
===============================================

.. folio:: introductio_arithmetica:0208
   :page: p. 192

Overview
--------

After opening with the general arithmetic-mean identity :math:`n = \frac{1}{2}((n-k) + (n+k))` and proving the primacy of the :term:`monad <monas>` (:math:`1 = \frac{1}{2}(2)`), Nicomachus introduces the tripartition of **even** numbers and analyzes the first extreme species—the **even-times even** (*artiakis artios*, powers of two :math:`2^k`):

1. **Continuous Bisection to Unity (I.8.4–7)**: An even-times even number can be halved repeatedly, along with every resulting part, all the way down to the indivisible monad. Every proper divisor and quotient is itself a power of two.
2. **Generation & Reciprocal Divisor Pairing (I.8.8–11)**: All even-times even numbers are generated exclusively by doubling from unity (:math:`1, 2, 4, 8, \dots, 2^k`). In any truncated progression up to :math:`2^k`, complementary divisors pair symmetrically from the center outward (:math:`2^j \cdot 2^{k-j} = 2^k`): two central partners when the term count :math:`k+1` is even, or one self-paired square-root mean when :math:`k+1` is odd.
3. **Geometric-Series Sum Identity (I.8.12–13)**: Every partial sum :math:`1 + 2 + 4 + \dots + 2^{k-1} = 2^k - 1` is odd and falls short of the next power of two by unity—the key lemma later used in Chapter 16 to generate :term:`perfect numbers <teleios>`.
4. **Equal-Products Law (I.8.14)**: Across symmetric extremes, :math:`a_0 a_k = a_1 a_{k-1} = \dots`, equaling the product of the two central terms or the square of the single central term.
