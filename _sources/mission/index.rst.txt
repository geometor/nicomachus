Mission
=======

.. rubric:: Why Nicomachus of Gerasa Anchors Arithmetic, Harmonics, and the Quadrivium in GEOMETOR

Between the axiomatic deduction of :person:`Euclid's <euclid>` *Elements* (Books VII–IX) and the medieval Latin university curriculum initiated by :person:`Boethius <boethius>`, :person:`Nicomachus of Gerasa <nicomachus>` (c. 60–120 CE) composed the single treatise that defined *number* as the intelligible exemplar in the Mind of the Creator (:cite:`hoche:1866`, I.6.1) and unified discrete arithmetic with musical consonance, polygonal form, and three-dimensional polyhedral symmetry.

   :alt: Nicomachus's Ontological Bifurcation of Quantity into the Four Mathematical Sciences
   :caption: Book I, Chs. 3–5: Quantity divides into discrete Multitude (poson) and continuous Magnitude (pēlikon), generating the four sister sciences—Arithmetic, Music, Geometry, and Sphaerics/Astronomy—ordered by asymmetric ontological priority (synanairein).

Four Pillars of ``geometor/nicomachus``
---------------------------------------

1. **The Ontological Charter of the Quadrivium (Book I, Chs. 1–6)**:
   Quoting :person:`Archytas of Tarentum <archytas>` (:cite:`archytas:dk47b1`) and :person:`Plato's <plato>` *Timaeus* and *Republic* VII (:cite:`plato:rep:vii-525a-534e`), Nicomachus proves that :g:`Quantity <quadrivium-tetrad:QUANT>` bifurcates into discrete :g:`Multitude <quadrivium-tetrad:MULT>` (:term:`poson <poson>`) and continuous :g:`Magnitude <quadrivium-tetrad:MAGN>` (:term:`pelikon <pelikon>`). Multitude considered in itself yields :g:`Arithmetic <quadrivium-tetrad:ARITH>`, while multitude in relation yields :g:`Music <quadrivium-tetrad:MUSIC>`; magnitude at rest yields :g:`Geometry <quadrivium-tetrad:GEOM>`, while magnitude in revolution yields :g:`Astronomy <quadrivium-tetrad:ASTRON>`. By :person:`Aristotle's <aristotle>` criterion of :term:`asymmetric co-removal <synanairein>`, :g:`Arithmetic precedes all three sister sciences <quadrivium-tetrad:PRIORITY>` as their "mother" (*mētēr*).
2. **Every Inequality Unfolded from Equality (Book I, Ch. 23 & Book II, Chs. 1–2)**:
   Through the three commands (*prostagmata*) :math:`(a, b, c) \mapsto (a, a+b, a+2b+c)` combined with sibling *anastrophe* reversal :math:`(c, b, a)`, Nicomachus demonstrates that every rational ratio—multiple, superparticular, superpartient, and compound—streams forth deterministically from a single root of :term:`Equality <isotes>` :math:`(1, 1, 1)`, and reduces uniquely back to :math:`(1, 1, 1)` under inverse subtraction.
3. **Figurate Alpha-Gnomons, Same/Other & Sum of Cubes (Book II, Chs. 6–20)**:
   Representing units by alpha-pebbles (``α``), Nicomachus builds all :math:`s`-gonal numbers by arithmetic :term:`gnomons <gnomon>` of common difference :math:`s - 2`, weaves :person:`Plato's <plato>` *Same* (:math:`n^2`) and *Other* (:math:`n(n+1)`) into universal arithmetic harmonies, and discovers that successive blocks of odd numbers sum to cubes :math:`k^3` whose cumulative area equals the squared triangular number :math:`T_n^2`.
4. **The Ten Means & the Cube's Most Perfect Proportion (Book II, Chs. 21–29)**:
   Expanding the three classical means (Arithmetic, Geometric, Harmonic) into the ten Neo-Pythagorean proportions, Nicomachus culminates in :math:`6 : 8 :: 9 : 12`—where the **Cube's** :math:`6` faces, :math:`8` vertices, and :math:`12` edges encode the harmonic (:math:`8`) and arithmetic (:math:`9`) means and every Pythagorean consonance (:math:`2:1, 3:2, 4:3, 9:8`).
