Chapter 2: Eternal Being vs. Bodily Flux; Magnitude & Multitude — Summary
=========================================================================

.. folio:: introductio_arithmetica:0198
   :page: p. 182

Overview
--------

Building on the distinction between immaterial realities and sensible bodies, Nicomachus cites :person:`Plato's <plato>` *Timaeus* (27d–28a) and introduces the fundamental dichotomy of mathematical objects:

1. **Continuous Magnitude (*Megethos*) vs. Discrete Multitude (*Plēthos*) (I.2.4)**: Unified wholes (an animal, the cosmos, a tree) are *magnitudes*, whereas juxtaposed, discontinuous collections (a flock, a populace, a heap, a chorus) are *multitudes*.
2. **Limiting the Infinite for Scientific Knowledge (I.2.5)**: Multitude *per se* increases without bound toward the greater, while magnitude *per se* divides without bound toward the less. Because science (*episteme*) only grasps bounded objects, mathematics arises by delimiting finite :term:`quantity <poson>` (*to poson*) from multitude and finite :term:`size <pelikon>` (*to pēlikon*) from magnitude.
