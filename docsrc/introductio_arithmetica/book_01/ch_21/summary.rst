=================================================================================
Chapter 21: Generation of Superpartients & Overview of Compound Species — Summary
=================================================================================

.. folio:: introductio_arithmetica:0237
   :page: p. 221

Overview
--------

Nicomachus constructs the canonical table of superpartient ratios and introduces the transition from the three simple species to the two compound species:

1. **Generating the Root-Forms (I.21.1)**: Pairing the pure odd numbers from :math:`5` (:math:`5, 7, 9, 11, 13, \dots`) with all integers from :math:`3` (:math:`3, 4, 5, 6, 7, \dots`) yields the root-forms (*pythmenes*) :math:`5:3, 7:4, 9:5, 11:6, 13:7`, and scaling both terms by :math:`2, 3, 4, \dots` fills out each species column.
2. **Implicit Denominators in Greek Nomenclature (I.21.2)**: Saying *epiditritos* ("two-thirds") or *epitritetartos* ("three-fourths") automatically supplies the denominator one greater than the numerator of the excess parts (:math:`1 + \frac{2}{3}, 1 + \frac{3}{4}, 1 + \frac{4}{5}, 1 + \frac{5}{6}`).
3. **Transition to Compound Relations (I.21.3)**: Having finished the three simple species, Nicomachus introduces the two species woven out of multiple and fractional excess: :g:`multiple superparticular <five-species-ratios:SP4>` and :g:`multiple superpartient <five-species-ratios:SP5>`, with their `sub-` reciprocals.
