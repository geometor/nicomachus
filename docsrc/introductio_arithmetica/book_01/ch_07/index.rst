==============================================
Chapter 7: Definition of Number, Even, and Odd
==============================================

.. folio:: introductio_arithmetica:0206
   :page: p. 190

**1.** :term:`Number <arithmos>` is a bounded multitude, or an ordered assembly of :term:`monads <monas>`, or a flowing stream of quantity composed of monads. The first division of number is into the **even** and the **odd**.

**2.** **Even** is that which admits division into two equal parts without a :term:`monad <monas>` falling between in the middle; **odd** is that which cannot be parted into two equal halves because of the aforementioned mediation of the monad.

**3.** This definition, then, comes from popular opinion. According to the Pythagorean doctrine, however, an **even** number is that which admits at one and the same stroke a cut into the greatest and the smallest parts—greatest in size, smallest in multitude—

.. folio:: introductio_arithmetica:0207
   :page: p. 191

by the natural reciprocal opposition of these two kinds; whereas an **odd** number is that which cannot undergo this, being cut instead into two unequal parts.

**4.** In another ancient formulation, an **even** number is that which can be cut both into two equal parts and into two unequal parts—except for the primal **dyad** within it, which admits only the one bisection into equals—and which in any cut displays only one of the two species of number at a time, however it be divided, unmixed with the other; while an **odd** number is that which in every cut—which is always into unequal parts—displays at once both species of number, never unblended with each other, but always together.

**5.** And by the mutual definition of each through the other, an **odd** number is that which differs by a monad on either side from an even number—that is, toward the greater and toward the less—while an **even** number is that which differs by a monad on either side from an odd number, being greater by a monad and less by a monad.
