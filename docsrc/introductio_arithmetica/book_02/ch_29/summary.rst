===================================================================
Chapter 29: The Most Perfect Proportion (6 : 8 :: 9 : 12) — Summary
===================================================================

.. folio:: introductio_arithmetica:0300
   :page: p. 284

Overview
--------

Nicomachus concludes the *Introduction to Arithmetic* with the "most perfect" three-dimensional proportion :math:`6, 8, 9, 12`, uniting arithmetic, harmonic, and geometric proportionality inside solid numbers:

1. **Three-Dimensional Solid Realization (II.29.1–3)**: Between the solid extremes $6 = 1 \times 2 \times 3$ and $12 = 2 \times 2 \times 3$ lie the :g:`harmonic mean 8 = 1 × 2 × 4 <cube-most-perfect-proportion:VERTS8>` and the arithmetic mean $9 = 1 \times 3 \times 3$.
2. **Interwoven Means**: $(6, 8, 12)$ is harmonic, $(6, 9, 12)$ is arithmetic, and $6 : 8 :: 9 : 12$ ($3 : 4$) and $6 : 9 :: 8 : 12$ ($2 : 3$) form disjunct geometric proportions ($6 \times 12 = 8 \times 9 = 72$).
3. **Diatonic Scale Scaffolding**: On the :g:`monochord <cube-most-perfect-proportion:MONOCHORD>`, $6, 8, 9, 12$ yields the :g:`fourth (4:3) <cube-most-perfect-proportion:FOURTH>`, :g:`fifth (3:2) <cube-most-perfect-proportion:FIFTH>`, :g:`octave (2:1) <cube-most-perfect-proportion:OCTAVE>`, and central :g:`whole tone (9:8) <cube-most-perfect-proportion:TONE>`.
