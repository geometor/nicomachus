====================================================================
Chapter 21: Nature of Proportion, Ratio, Continuous & Disjunct Forms
====================================================================

.. folio:: introductio_arithmetica:0280
   :page: p. 264

**1.** After this it would be the proper time to incorporate the nature of :term:`proportions <analogia>`, a thing most essential for speculation about the nature of the universe and for the propositions of music, astronomy, and geometry, and not least for the study of the works of the ancients, and thus to bring the *Introduction to Arithmetic* to the end that is at once suitable and fitting.

**2.** A :term:`proportion <analogia>`, then, is in the proper sense, the combination of two or more *ratios*, but by the more general definition the combination of

.. folio:: introductio_arithmetica:0281
   :page: p. 265

two or more *relations*, even if they are not brought under the same ratio, but rather a difference, or something else.

**3.** Now a ratio is the relation of two terms to one another, and the combination of such is a proportion, so that three is the smallest number of terms of which the latter is composed, although it can be a series of more, subject to the same ratio or the same difference. For example, :math:`1 : 2` is one ratio, where there are two terms; but :math:`2 : 4` is another similar ratio; hence :math:`1, 2, 4` is a proportion, for it is a combination of ratios, or of three terms which are observed to be in the same ratio to one another.

**4.** The same thing may be observed also in greater numbers and longer series of terms; for let a fourth term, :math:`8`, be joined to the former after :math:`4`, again in a similar relation, the double, and then :math:`16` after :math:`8` and so on.

**5.** Now if the same term, one and unchanging, is compared to those on

.. folio:: introductio_arithmetica:0282
   :page: p. 266

either side of it, to the greater as consequent and to the lesser as antecedent, such a proportion is called continued; for example, :math:`1, 2, 4` is a continued proportion as regards quality, for :math:`4 : 2` equals :math:`2 : 1`, and conversely :math:`1 : 2` equals :math:`2 : 4`. In quantity, :math:`1, 2, 3`, for example, is a continued proportion, for as :math:`3` exceeds :math:`2`, so :math:`2` exceeds :math:`1`, and conversely, as :math:`1` is less than :math:`2`, by so much :math:`2` is less than :math:`3`.

**6.** If, however, one term answers to the lesser term, and becomes its antecedent and a greater term, and another, not the same, takes the place of consequent and lesser term with reference to the greater, such a mean and such a proportion is called no longer continued, but disjunct; for example, as regards quality, :math:`1, 2, 4, 8`, for :math:`2 : 1` equals :math:`8 : 4`, and conversely :math:`1 : 2` equals :math:`4 : 8`, and again :math:`1 : 4` equals :math:`2 : 8` or :math:`4 : 1` equals :math:`8 : 2`; and in quantity, :math:`1, 2, 3, 4`, for as :math:`1` is exceeded by :math:`2`, so :math:`3` is exceeded by :math:`4`, or as :math:`4` exceeds :math:`3`, so :math:`2` exceeds :math:`1`, and by interchange, as :math:`3` exceeds :math:`1`, so :math:`4` exceeds :math:`2`, or as :math:`1` is exceeded by :math:`3`, by so much :math:`2` is exceeded by :math:`4`.
