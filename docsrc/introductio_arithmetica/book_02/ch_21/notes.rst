Philological & Mathematical Notes
---------------------------------

* **Analogia vs. Mesotēs**: As :person:`D'Ooge <dooge>` and :person:`Robbins <robbins>` observe (:cite:`dooge:1926`, p. 264 n. 2), earlier writers distinguished *analogia* (strictly geometric proportion, *kata poiotēta*, cf. Euclid *Elements* V Def. 3–6) from *mesotēs* ("mean", including arithmetic and harmonic progressions). Nicomachus explicitly allows *analogia* in the broad sense to cover both ratio-based and difference-based progressions while preserving the strict sense for geometric proportion (Ch. 24.1).
* **Definition of Ratio (*Logos*, II.21.3)**: Nicomachus defines *logos* simply as *dyo horōn pros allēlous schesis* ("relation of two terms to one another"). Compare :person:`Euclid <euclid>` (*Elements* V Def. 3: *logos esti dyo megethōn homogenōn hē kata pēlikotēta poia schesis*) and :person:`Theon of Smyrna <theon-of-smyrna>` (p. 73 Hiller), who specify *homogeneous* magnitudes and *qualitative* relation (*poia schesis*) to distinguish geometric ratio from quantitative arithmetic difference (*posē schesis*).
* **Continuous (*Synēmmenē*) vs. Disjunct (*Diezeugmenē*)**: The same distinction appears in :person:`Theon of Smyrna <theon-of-smyrna>` (p. 82 Hiller, using *synechēs* and *diērēmenē*) and in :person:`Boethius <boethius>` (*continua* vs. *disiuncta*).
