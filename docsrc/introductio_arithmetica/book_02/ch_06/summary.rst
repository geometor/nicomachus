Overview
--------

Nicomachus turns from relative quantity to the geometric figuration of absolute numbers, laying down the foundations of dimensional arithmetic:

1. **Priority of Arithmetic over Geometry (II.6.1)**: Linear, plane, and solid figures belong properly to geometry, yet their germinal principles lie in arithmetic, the older and foundational science.
2. **Natural Alpha-Pebble Notation (II.6.2–3)**: Conventional alphabetic numerals (:math:`\iota = 10, \kappa = 20, \omega = 800`) exist by human convention, whereas representing each unit by a single ``α`` placed side by side (``α``, ``αα``, ``ααα``, ``αααα``, ``ααααα``) reveals geometric structure naturally.
3. **The Monad and the Three Dimensions (II.6.4–7)**: The :term:`monad <monas>` corresponds to the dimensionless **point** (source of intervals without itself having interval, just as :math:`1 \times 1 = 1`). Interval appears first in :math:`2`, giving one dimension (**line**), two dimensions (**surface**), and at most three dimensions (**solid**: length, breadth, depth), governed by the six spatial directions (*up/down, forward/back, right/left*).
