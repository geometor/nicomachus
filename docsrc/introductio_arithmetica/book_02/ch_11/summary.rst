=========================================================================
Chapter 11: Hexagonal, Heptagonal, and Higher Polygonal Numbers — Summary
=========================================================================

.. folio:: introductio_arithmetica:0261
   :page: p. 245

Overview
--------

Nicomachus generalizes the generation of :term:`polygonal numbers <polygonos>` beyond triangles, squares, and pentagons to hexagons, heptagons, octagons, and arbitrary $s$-gons, culminating in his universal :term:`gnomon <gnomon>` difference law:

1. **Hexagonal Numbers (II.11.1)**: Summing arithmetic terms differing by $4$ (skipping three terms at a time in the natural series: $1, 5, 9, 13, 17, 21, \dots$) yields $1, 6, 15, 28, 45, 66, \dots$
2. **Heptagonal & Octagonal Numbers (II.11.2–3)**: Summing terms differing by $5$ (skipping four: $1, 6, 11, 16, 21, \dots$) produces heptagonals $1, 7, 18, 34, 55, 81, 112, 148, \dots$; a common difference of $6$ yields octagonals $1, 8, 21, 40, 65, \dots$
3. **Universal Gnomon Difference Law (II.11.4)**: For any $s$-gonal number, the common difference $d$ between consecutive gnomons is always two less than the number of angles: $d = s - 2$, giving the closed formula $P(s, n) = n + (s - 2)\frac{n(n - 1)}{2}$.
