Chapter 4: Triple Ratio Diagram and Natural Order of Multiples — Summary
========================================================================

.. folio:: introductio_arithmetica:0249
   :page: p. 233

Overview
--------

Continuing the construction of Chapter 3, Nicomachus constructs the diagram for triple ratios and draws a general law of priority among multiples:

1. **Triples and Sesquitertians (II.4.1–2)**: Along the top row of triples :math:`1, 3, 9, 27, 81, 243, 729`, each term :math:`3^k` heads exactly :math:`k` consecutive :term:`sesquitertian <epitritos>` (:math:`4:3`) steps down its column, halting at :math:`4^k`, which admits no integer third.
2. **Hypotenuse Progression Law (II.4.3–4)**: In every diagram whose horizontal rows are :math:`m`-fold multiples and whose columns descend by superparticular ratio :math:`(m+1):m`, the diagonal hypotenuse inevitably manifests the next higher multiple species :math:`(m+1)`-fold (doubles yield triples along the diagonal; triples yield quadruples; quadruples yield quintuples)—confirming that lower multiples are naturally prior to higher ones.
