Chapter 26: Musical Consonances & Philolaus's Cube as Geometric Harmony — Summary

.. folio:: introductio_arithmetica:0293
   :page: p. 277

Overview
--------

Nicomachus links the harmonic proportion to Pythagorean acoustics and solid geometry:

1. **Five Canonical Consonances (II.26.1)**: All five Greek musical concords appear in $3, 4, 6$ and $2, 3, 6$ (either directly between terms or between terms and differences): the fourth ($4:3$), fifth ($3:2$), octave ($2:1$), octave-plus-fifth ($3:1$), and double octave ($4:1$).
2. **Philolaus's Cube as Geometric Harmony (II.26.2)**: Every cube has $6$ faces, $8$ vertices, and $12$ edges. Because $12 : 6 = (12 - 8) : (8 - 6)$, the triplet $(6, 8, 12)$ forms a harmonic proportion encoding the fourth ($8:6$), fifth ($12:8$), and octave ($12:6$).
