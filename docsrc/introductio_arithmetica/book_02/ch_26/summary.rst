Chapter 26: Musical Consonances & Philolaus's Cube as Geometric Harmony — Summary
=================================================================================

.. folio:: introductio_arithmetica:0293
   :page: p. 277

Overview
--------

Nicomachus links the harmonic proportion to Pythagorean acoustics and solid geometry:

1. **Five Canonical Consonances (II.26.1)**: All five Greek musical concords appear in :math:`3, 4, 6` and :math:`2, 3, 6` (either directly between terms or between terms and differences): the fourth (:math:`4:3`), fifth (:math:`3:2`), octave (:math:`2:1`), octave-plus-fifth (:math:`3:1`), and double octave (:math:`4:1`).
2. **Philolaus's Cube as Geometric Harmony (II.26.2)**: Every cube has :math:`6` faces, :math:`8` vertices, and :math:`12` edges. Because :math:`12 : 6 = (12 - 8) : (8 - 6)`, the triplet :math:`(6, 8, 12)` forms a harmonic proportion encoding the fourth (:math:`8:6`), fifth (:math:`12:8`), and octave (:math:`12:6`).
