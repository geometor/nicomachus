Chapter 20: Generation of Cubes from Consecutive Odd Blocks — Summary
=====================================================================

.. folio:: introductio_arithmetica:0277
   :page: p. 261

Overview
--------

Nicomachus concludes the figurate-number treatise by proving four properties linking squares, heteromecics, geometric progressions, and cubes to the odd principle of the *Same*:

1. **Square-to-Heteromecic Shifts (II.20.1–4)**: :math:`n^2 \pm n` yields adjacent heteromecics; paired terms trade equal differences for equal ratios, with common difference :math:`d` being :math:`\frac{1}{m}` of one term and :math:`\frac{1}{m+1}` of the other.
2. **Squares in Geometric Progressions (II.20.5)**: In any geometric progression from :math:`1` (:math:`1, r, r^2, r^3, \dots`), precisely the odd-indexed terms are squares.
3. **Nicomachus's Odd-Block Cube Theorem (II.20.5)**: Partitioning the odd numbers into consecutive blocks of size :math:`1, 2, 3, 4, \dots` generates every cube :math:`k^3`, laying the foundation for :math:`\sum_{k=1}^n k^3 = T_n^2`.
