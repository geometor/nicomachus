====================================================================
Chapter 3: Multiples Generating Corresponding Superparticular Ratios
====================================================================

.. folio:: introductio_arithmetica:0248
   :page: p. 232

**1.** Every :term:`multiple <pollaplasios>` stands at the head of as many :term:`superparticular <epimorios>` ratios named opposite to it as is its own rank from unity, and by no device either more or fewer. Doubles, then, will bring forth :term:`sesquialters <hemiolios>`: the first one, the second two, the third three, the fourth four, the fifth five, the sixth six, and neither more nor fewer;
rather, by complete necessity, whenever the superparticulars generated attain the commensurate number equal in multitude to the multiples that generated them, then by some marvelous necessity the number terminating them all is found by nature incapable of admitting that fractional part by which the superparticulars were advancing. From the triples all the :term:`sesquitertians <epitritos>` will advance in the same way, equal in multitude to their generators and ending, once their progression is complete, at numbers admitting no third part; and likewise the sesquiquartans from the quadruples find their summit in a number admitting no fourth part after their due progression.

**2.** For example, when doubles generate an equal number of sesquialters, the top row of multiples will be first: :math:`1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64`.
Since :math:`2` is first after unity, it will originate only one sesquialter, :math:`3`, which cannot admit a half so as to yield another sesquialter. Thus the first double generates only one sesquialter; the second, :math:`4`, generates two sesquialters—for its own is :math:`6`, and that of :math:`6` is :math:`9`, while :math:`9` has no other since it lacks a half. The third double, :math:`8`, is father of three sesquialters: :math:`12` to it, :math:`18` to :math:`12`, and :math:`27` to :math:`18`, yet no fourth by the universal rule, for :math:`27` admits no half. The fourth double, :math:`16`, heads four sesquialters: :math:`24, 36, 54`, and :math:`81` last, since :math:`81` admits no half; and advancing indefinitely you will find it so in like proportion.
