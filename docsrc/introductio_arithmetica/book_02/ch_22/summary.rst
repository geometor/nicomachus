===================================================================================
Chapter 22: The Ten Proportions & Natural Priority of the Arithmetic Mean — Summary
===================================================================================

.. folio:: introductio_arithmetica:0282
   :page: p. 266

Overview
--------

Nicomachus introduces the complete Pythagorean Decad of ten means and explains why the arithmetic mean holds natural precedence over all others:

1. **The Decad of Proportions (II.22.1)**: Three classical proportions—arithmetic, geometric, and :term:`harmonic <harmonike>`—are universally recognized by :person:`Pythagoras <pythagoras>`, :person:`Plato <plato>`, and :person:`Aristotle <aristotle>`. Three :term:`subcontrary <hypenantia>` means (4th, 5th, 6th) stand opposite them, and four later discoveries complete the sacred Pythagorean number ten, matching the ten ratio-relations, the ten Aristotelian categories, and the ten digits of hands and feet.
2. **Double Priority of the Arithmetic Mean (II.22.2–4)**: First, Nature exhibits the arithmetic proportion immediately in the unbroken natural sequence $1, 2, 3, 4, \dots$ starting from the :term:`monad <monas>`. Second, by the ontological co-removal test (:term:`synanairein`, cf. Book I, Ch. 4), arithmetic itself precedes geometry and harmonics; hence the mean named after arithmetic rightly precedes the geometric and harmonic means, and *a fortiori* all their subcontrary derivatives.
