Book I, Ch. 17 — Equality & the Five Species of Relative Inequality
-------------------------------------------------------------------

*English Translation by Martin Luther D'Ooge (1926) · Hoche pp. 44–46 · D'Ooge pp. 212–214*

.. folio:: D'Ooge p. 212 · Hoche p. 44

**1–2.** Now that we have given a preliminary systematic account of **absolute quantity** (*to kath' heauto poson*)—even, odd, prime, composite, superabundant, deficient, and perfect—it is next in turn to examine **relative quantity** (*to pros ti poson*). Of relative quantity there are two highest generic divisions: :term:`Equality <isotes>` (*isotēs*) and **Inequality** (*anisotēs*). For everything viewed in comparison with another thing is either equal to it or unequal, and there is no third besides these.

.. folio:: D'Ooge p. 213 · Hoche p. 45

**3–5.** :g:`Equality (1 : 1) <five-species-ratios:EQ11>` occurs when of the things compared neither exceeds nor falls short of the other, as a hundred to a hundred, ten to ten, two to two, a mina to a mina, a talent to a talent, a cubit to a cubit; and as a peculiar property this relation is **natural and indivisible**, incapable of being split into subspecies—for there is not one kind of equality and another kind, since equality remains one and identical.

**6–8.** The **unequal**, on the other hand, is split by division, and one part of it is the **greater** (*meizon*), the other the **less** (*elatton*), which have opposite names and relations to one another. Again, the **greater** is subdivided into **five species**, and the **less** correspondingly into five opposite (*hypenantia*) species:

1. :g:`Multiple (pollaplasios) <five-species-ratios:SP1>` — opposite to **submultiple** (*hypopollaplasios*)
2. :g:`Superparticular (epimorios) <five-species-ratios:SP2>` — opposite to **subsuperparticular** (*hypepimorios*)
3. :g:`Superpartient (epimerēs) <five-species-ratios:SP3>` — opposite to **subsuperpartient** (*hypepimerēs*)
4. :g:`Multiple superparticular (pollaplasiepimorios) <five-species-ratios:SP4>` — opposite to **submultiple-superparticular**
5. :g:`Multiple superpartient (pollaplasiepimerēs) <five-species-ratios:SP5>` — opposite to **submultiple-superpartient**

.. _nicomachus-en-1-18:

Book I, Ch. 18 — First Species: The Multiple (*Pollaplasios*)
-------------------------------------------------------------

*Hoche pp. 46–49 · D'Ooge pp. 214–216*

.. folio:: D'Ooge p. 214 · Hoche p. 46

**1–4.** Again, then, the :g:`multiple <five-species-ratios:SP1>` (:term:`pollaplasios <pollaplasios>`) is the first and most original by nature of all the species of the greater, as straightway we shall see, and it is a number which, when observed in comparison with another, contains the whole of that number **more than once** (:math:`m \cdot b : b` for :math:`m \ge 2`). For instance, compared with unity, all the successive numbers beginning with :math:`2` generate the regular forms of multiplicity in turn: :math:`2` in the first place is and is called **double** (*diplasiōn*), :math:`3` **triple** (*triplasiōn*), :math:`4` **quadruple** (*tetraplasiōn*), :math:`5` **quintuple**, and so on indefinitely; and opposite to these stand *half* (*hēmisys*), *third* (*tritos*), *fourth* (*tetartos*), and so on.

.. _nicomachus-en-1-19:

Book I, Ch. 19 — Second Species: The Superparticular (*Epimorios*) & Musical Consonances
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

*Hoche pp. 49–55 · D'Ooge pp. 216–219*

.. folio:: D'Ooge p. 216 · Hoche p. 49

**1–3.** The second species of the greater, both naturally and in order, is the :g:`superparticular <five-species-ratios:SP2>` (:term:`epimorios <epimorios>`), which contains within itself the whole of the number compared **plus some one aliquot factor** (*morion*) of that lesser number (:math:`(n + 1) : n` for :math:`n \ge 2`):

* If this factor is **half**, the greater of the terms compared is called specifically :term:`sesquialter <hemiolios>` (*hēmiolios*, :math:`3:2, 6:4, 9:6, 12:8`), governing the musical **fifth** (*diapente*).
* If it is a **third**, it is called :term:`sesquitertian <epitritos>` (*epitritos*, :math:`4:3, 8:6, 12:9`), governing the musical **fourth** (*diatessaron*).
* If it is a **fourth**, *epitetartos* (:math:`5:4`); if an **eighth**, :term:`sesquioctave <epogdoos>` (*epogdoos*, :math:`9:8`), governing the **whole tone** (*tonos*) by which the fifth exceeds the fourth (:math:`\frac{3}{2} \div \frac{4}{3} = \frac{9}{8}`).

.. folio:: D'Ooge p. 217 · Hoche p. 51

**4–9.** Observe the wonderful order of nature: when the natural series from :math:`1` is compared with the **even** series :math:`2, 4, 6, 8, \dots` vs. the **triple** series :math:`3, 6, 9, 12, \dots`, the root terms (:math:`3:2, 4:3, 5:4, \dots`) admit no intervening integer and reveal the pure root ratios, while their multiples (:math:`6:4, 9:6, 12:8, \dots`) preserve the exact same relation across an infinite multiplication table.

Book I, Chs. 20–22 — Superpartient & the Two Compound Ratio Species
-------------------------------------------------------------------

*Hoche pp. 55–64 · D'Ooge pp. 219–225*

.. folio:: D'Ooge p. 219 · Hoche p. 55

**I.20 (Superpartient / Epimerēs).** We have the :g:`superpartient relation <five-species-ratios:SP3>` (:term:`epimeres <epimeres>`) when a number contains within itself the whole of the number compared **and in addition more than one aliquot part of it** (:math:`(n + r) : n` where :math:`1 < r < n` and :math:`\gcd(n, r) = 1`)—beginning with **two** parts (*epidimerēs* / superbipartient, :math:`5:3` or *disepitetartos* in the looser speech for :math:`5:3` as :math:`3 + 2`), **three** parts (*epitrimerēs* / supertripartient, :math:`7:4`), **four** parts (:math:`9:5`), and in general comparing successive odd numbers from :math:`5` (:math:`5, 7, 9, 11, \dots`) with consecutive integers from :math:`3` (:math:`3, 4, 5, 6, \dots`).

.. folio:: D'Ooge p. 221 · Hoche p. 58

**I.21–22 (Multiple Superparticular & Multiple Superpartient).** From the blending of the simple species arise the two compound classes:

* The :g:`multiple superparticular <five-species-ratios:SP4>` (:term:`pollaplasiepimorios <pollaplasiepimorios>`) occurs when the greater term contains the lesser **more than once plus one aliquot part** (:math:`(m \cdot n + 1) : n` with :math:`m \ge 2, n \ge 2`), such as *duplex sesquialter* (*diplasiepēmiolios*, :math:`5:2 = 2 \cdot 2 + 1 : 2`), *duplex sesquitertian* (:math:`7:3`), or *triplex sesquialter* (:math:`7:2`).
* The :g:`multiple superpartient <five-species-ratios:SP5>` (*pollaplasiepimerēs*) occurs when the greater term contains the lesser **more than once plus several aliquot parts** (:math:`(m \cdot n + r) : n` with :math:`m \ge 2, 1 < r < n, \gcd(n, r) = 1`), such as *duplex superbipartient* (:math:`8:3 = 2 \cdot 3 + 2 : 3`) or *duplex supertripartient* (:math:`11:4`).

Book I, Ch. 23 — The Three *Prostagma* Commands Unfolding All Ratios from Equality
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------

*Hoche pp. 64–72 · D'Ooge pp. 225–229*

.. picture:: /_static/figures/equality-unfolding-tree.svg
   :alt: Nicomachus's Ternary Unfolding Tree from Equality (1, 1, 1)
   :caption: Book I, Ch. 23 & Book II, Chs. 1–2: Unfolding all five species of relative inequality from primordial Equality (1, 1, 1) by the direct command (a, a+b, a+2b+c) and sibling reversal (c, b, a).

.. folio:: D'Ooge p. 226 · Hoche p. 65

**I.23.4–8 (The Three Commands).** That the whole system of species in relations proceeds from :term:`equality <isotes>` as its first and elementary root will become indisputable by the following graceful method. Let there be given to us three terms in **equality**—either three units :g:`(1, 1, 1) <equality-unfolding-tree:ROOT111>`, or three twos :math:`(2, 2, 2)`, or three threes, or any three equal numbers whatever—and from them let three new terms be molded according to **three unvarying commands** (*tria prostagmata*) as if by a law of nature:

1. **Make the first equal to the first**: :math:`a' = a`
2. **Make the second equal to the first plus the second**: :math:`b' = a + b`
3. **Make the third equal to the first, plus twice the second, plus the third**: :math:`c' = a + 2b + c`

.. folio:: D'Ooge p. 227 · Hoche p. 67

**I.23.8–15 (Generation of Every Species in Exact Order).** Watch how every species of inequality springs forth in natural sequence without our contrivance:

* **Step 1 — All Multiples from Equality**: Apply the three commands to :g:`(1, 1, 1) <equality-unfolding-tree:ROOT111>`: first :math:`= 1`, second :math:`= 1 + 1 = 2`, third :math:`= 1 + 2(1) + 1 = 4`, producing the **double** progression :g:`(1, 2, 4) <equality-unfolding-tree:N124>`. Apply the same commands directly to :math:`(1, 2, 4)` without rearrangement, and out comes the **triple** :g:`(1, 3, 9) <equality-unfolding-tree:N139>`; from :math:`(1, 3, 9)` springs the **quadruple** :g:`(1, 4, 16) <equality-unfolding-tree:N1416>`, and so on forever through all multiples!
* **Step 2 — Superparticulars from Reversed Multiples (*Anastrophe*)**: Now take those very multiples and reverse their order (:term:`anastrophe <anastrophe>`, placing the greatest term first). From reversed double :math:`(4, 2, 1)`, the three commands yield :math:`4,\; 4+2=6,\; 4+4+1=9`, namely :g:`(4, 6, 9) <equality-unfolding-tree:N469>`—the first **superparticular** (:term:`hemiolios <hemiolios>`, :math:`3:2`)! From reversed triple :math:`(9, 3, 1)` springs :g:`(9, 12, 16) <equality-unfolding-tree:N91216>` (:term:`epitritos <epitritos>`, :math:`4:3`); from reversed quadruple :math:`(16, 4, 1)` springs :math:`(16, 20, 25)` (*epitetartos*, :math:`5:4`), and so on!
* **Step 3 — Compound & Superpartient Species from Superparticulars**: From the superparticulars thus born, applying the three commands **directly** (starting from the least term) generates the **multiple superparticulars**—for :math:`(4, 6, 9)` directly yields :g:`(4, 10, 25) <equality-unfolding-tree:N41025>` (*duplex sesquialter*, :math:`5:2`), and :math:`(9, 12, 16)` yields :math:`(9, 21, 49)` (*duplex sesquitertian*, :math:`7:3`)—whereas applying the three commands to the **reversed** superparticulars (:math:`(9, 6, 4)` and :math:`(16, 12, 9)`) generates the **superpartients**: :g:`(9, 15, 25) <equality-unfolding-tree:N91525>` (*superbipartient*, :math:`5:3`) and :math:`(16, 28, 49)` (*supertripartient*, :math:`7:4`), while direct children of superpartients such as :math:`(9, 15, 25)` yield **multiple superpartients** like :g:`(9, 24, 64) <equality-unfolding-tree:N92464>` (:math:`8:3`)! Notice too the universal marvel: in every generated triple, **the extreme terms are invariably squares** (:math:`1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 49, 64, \dots`) surrounding their geometric mean!
