Chapter 10: Pentagonal Numbers and Gnomons Differing by Three — Summary¶

Overview¶

Nicomachus defines and constructs the pentagonal numbers, preparing the general polygonal rule of Chapter 11:

  1. Five-Angled Arrays (II.10.1–2): Pentagonal numbers (\(1, 5, 12, 22, 35, 51, 70, 92, 117, \dots\)) are five-angled equilateral plane figures whose sides advance by unity (\(1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, \dots\)) according to the number of summed terms.

  2. Gnomons Differing by Three (II.10.2–3): Skipping two consecutive integers at each step in the natural series selects the sequence \(1, 4, 7, 10, 13, 16, 19, 22, 25, \dots\) (common difference \(3\)); accumulating these gnomons produces \(1\), \(1+4=5\), \(5+7=12\), \(12+10=22\), \(22+13=35\), and so on via \(P(5, n) = \frac{n(3n - 1)}{2}\).
