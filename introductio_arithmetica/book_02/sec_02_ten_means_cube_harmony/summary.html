Unit IV — The Ten Neo-Pythagorean Means & the Cube’s Most Perfect Proportion — Structural Map¶

Semicircle Geometry, the Decad of Means & the Cube’s Teleiotatē Analogia · Book II, Chs. 21–29

Architectural Thesis¶

Unit IV crowns the Introductio Arithmetica by uniting arithmetic, geometry, stereometry, and harmonics in the theory of means:

  1. The Three Classical Means & Semicircle Identity (II.21–27): Arithmetic (\(A = \frac{a+c}{2}\)), Geometric (\(G = \sqrt{ac}\)), and Harmonic (\(H = \frac{2ac}{a+c}\)) means satisfy \(H \le G \le A\) and the exact geometric mean law \(G^2 = A \cdot H\), constructed on semicircle PR via radius OR = A, ordinate QS = G, and projection QF = H.

  2. The Complete Decad of Ten Proportions (II.28): Adding the three subcontrary means (4–6) and the four outer-difference means of Myonides and Euphranor (7–10) completes the Pythagorean Decad, culminating in Mean 10 (3, 5, 8) where \(a + b = c\).

  3. The Most Perfect Proportion 6 : 8 :: 9 : 12 (II.29): Built upon Philolaus's Cube (6 faces, 8 vertices, 12 edges), the four-term teleiotatē analogia embeds Arithmetic (\(9\)), Harmonic (\(8\)), and Geometric (\(6 \cdot 12 = 8 \cdot 9 = 72\)) symmetry alongside the octave 2:1, fifth 3:2, fourth 4:3, and whole tone 9:8.
