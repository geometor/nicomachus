Chapter 29: The Most Perfect Proportion (6 : 8 :: 9 : 12) — Summary¶

Overview¶

Nicomachus concludes the Introduction to Arithmetic with the “most perfect” three-dimensional proportion \(6, 8, 9, 12\), uniting arithmetic, harmonic, and geometric proportionality inside solid numbers:

  1. Three-Dimensional Solid Realization (II.29.1–3): Between the solid extremes \(6 = 1 \times 2 \times 3\) and \(12 = 2 \times 2 \times 3\) lie the harmonic mean 8 = 1 × 2 × 4 and the arithmetic mean \(9 = 1 \times 3 \times 3\).

  2. Interwoven Means: \((6, 8, 12)\) is harmonic, \((6, 9, 12)\) is arithmetic, and \(6 : 8 :: 9 : 12\) (\(3 : 4\)) and \(6 : 9 :: 8 : 12\) (\(2 : 3\)) form disjunct geometric proportions (\(6 \times 12 = 8 \times 9 = 72\)).

  3. Diatonic Scale Scaffolding: On the monochord, \(6, 8, 9, 12\) yields the fourth (4:3), fifth (3:2), octave (2:1), and central whole tone (9:8).
