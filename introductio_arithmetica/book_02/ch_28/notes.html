Chapter 28: The Seven Additional Means Completing the Ten Proportions — Notes¶

Archival Witnesses & Apparatus¶

  • English Witness: Martin Luther D’Ooge (trans.), Nicomachus of Gerasa: Introduction to Arithmetic (University of Michigan Studies, Vol. XVI, 1926), pp. 281–284 (folios introductio_arithmetica:0297–0300).

  • Greek Witness: Richard Hoche (ed.), Nicomachi Geraseni Pythagorei Introductionis Arithmeticae Libri II (Leipzig: Teubner, 1866), pp. 139–144 (folios hoche_1866:0158–0162).

  • Ancient Parallels: Pappus, Synagoge III.18–22 (pappus:synagoge:iii); Iamblichus, In Nicomachi Arithmeticam pp. 142, 162–168 Pistelli; Boethius, De Institutione Arithmetica II.51–53.

Robbins & D’Ooge Critical Notes on Means 8, 9 & 10¶

As Frank Egleston Robbins and Martin Luther D'Ooge establish in their critical notes (dooge:1926, pp. 282–284 nn.; cf. heath:hgm:1921, Vol. I, pp. 86–89), the manuscript tradition of Book II, Ch. 28.3–4 preserves two instructive slips in Nicomachus’s preliminary summary list (corrected in Ch. 28.9–10 and in Iamblichus / Pappus):

  1. 8th & 9th Means: In the preliminary list at II.28.3 (Hoche p. 140), the manuscripts give \(4, 7, 8\) and \(3, 5, 6\) alongside compressed verbal phrasing (“middle among the differences”), whereas in the detailed exposition at II.28.9–10 (Hoche pp. 142–143) the 8th mean \((c - a) : (c - b) = c : a\) is illustrated properly by 6, 7, 9 (\((9-6):(9-7) = 3:2 = 9:6\)) and the 9th mean \((c - a) : (b - a) = b : a\) is satisfied by 4, 6, 7 (\((7-4):(6-4) = 3:2 = 6:4\)). All ten canonical proportions and closed forms are verified symbolically in geometor.nicomachus.compute_all_ten_means and verify_ten_means_identities.

  2. 10th Mean as the Fibonacci Recurrence: The 10th mean \((c - a) : (c - b) = b : a\) expands to \(a(c - a) = b(c - b) \iff (b - a)(b + a - c) = 0\). Since \(a < b < c\) guarantees \(b \neq a\), this reduces identically to the Fibonacci additive law \(a + b = c\), which is why Nicomachus’s exemplar (3, 5, 8) consists of three consecutive Fibonacci numbers!

  3. Eudemus / Pappus Attribution: Pappus (Synagoge III.18–22) attributes the discovery of means 4–6 to Eudoxus (or Pythagoreans) and means 7–10 to later Neo-Pythagoreans such as Myonides and Euphranor (heath:hgm:1921, Vol. I, p. 86).
