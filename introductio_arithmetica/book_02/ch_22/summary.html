Chapter 22: The Ten Proportions & Natural Priority of the Arithmetic Mean — Summary¶

Overview¶

Nicomachus introduces the complete Pythagorean Decad of ten means and explains why the arithmetic mean holds natural precedence over all others:

  1. The Decad of Proportions (II.22.1): Three classical proportions—arithmetic, geometric, and harmonic—are universally recognized by Pythagoras, Plato, and Aristotle. Three subcontrary means (4th, 5th, 6th) stand opposite them, and four later discoveries complete the sacred Pythagorean number ten, matching the ten ratio-relations, the ten Aristotelian categories, and the ten digits of hands and feet.

  2. Double Priority of the Arithmetic Mean (II.22.2–4): First, Nature exhibits the arithmetic proportion immediately in the unbroken natural sequence $1, 2, 3, 4, \dots$ starting from the monad. Second, by the ontological co-removal test (synanairein, cf. Book I, Ch. 4), arithmetic itself precedes geometry and harmonics; hence the mean named after arithmetic rightly precedes the geometric and harmonic means, and a fortiori all their subcontrary derivatives.
