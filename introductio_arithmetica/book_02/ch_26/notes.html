Chapter 26: Musical Consonances & Philolaus’s Cube as Geometric Harmony — Notes¶

Archival Witnesses & Apparatus¶

  • English Witness: Martin Luther D’Ooge (trans.), Nicomachus of Gerasa: Introduction to Arithmetic (University of Michigan Studies, Vol. XVI, 1926), pp. 277–279 (folios introductio_arithmetica:0293–0295).

  • Greek Witness: Richard Hoche (ed.), Nicomachi Geraseni Pythagorei Introductionis Arithmeticae Libri II (Leipzig: Teubner, 1866), pp. 134–136 (folios hoche_1866:0152–0154).

  • Ancient Parallels: Philolaus Fr. A24 (DK 44A24); Cassiodorus, Institutiones II.4.7; Iamblichus, In Nicomachi Arithmeticam pp. 163–168 Pistelli.

Mathematical & Historical Notes¶

  • Philolaus’s Cube as Geometric Harmony: A cube has \(F = 6\) faces, \(V = 8\) vertices, and \(E = 12\) edges, satisfying Euler’s polyhedral invariant \(V - E + F = 8 - 12 + 6 = 2\) and the harmonic proportion \(\frac{1}{6} + \frac{1}{12} = \frac{2}{8} \iff H(6, 12) = 8\).

  • Octahedral Dual: Dually, the regular octahedron has \(V = 6, F = 8, E = 12\), preserving the identical harmonic triple \((6, 8, 12)\).

  • Difference Consonances: In \((3, 4, 6)\), differences are \(1, 2, 3\): \(6:2 = 3:1\) (twelfth) and \(4:1 = 4:1\) (double octave). In \((2, 3, 6)\), differences are \(1, 3, 4\): \(3:1\) (twelfth) and \(4:1\) (double octave).
