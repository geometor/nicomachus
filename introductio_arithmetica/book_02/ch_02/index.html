Chapter 2: Inverse Three-Subtraction Reduction to Equality (1, 1, 1)¶

1. When three terms are given to you in any relation and proportion whatever—whether multiple, superparticular, superpartient, or a compound of these, multiple superparticular or multiple superpartient—

f. 0247 · p. 231

provided only that the middle term stands in the same ratio to the lesser as the greater stands to the middle, or conversely, always subtract the least term from the middle, whether it stands first or last; set down that least term itself as first term, and place what remains from the second after the subtraction as second term; then, after subtracting one such first term and two such second terms from the remaining term—that is, from the greatest of the given numbers—make what is left the third term, and the resulting terms will stand

f. 0248 · p. 232

in another relation, naturally earlier in order. 2. Again, if from these very terms you subtract in the same manner what remains, the three terms will be led backward for you to three more fundamental terms; and you will find this always happening step by step until they are reduced to equality, from which it is manifestly necessary to declare that equality is in every respect the element of relative quantity.

3. This theory is accompanied by a most harmonious theorem, exceedingly useful both for Plato’s generation of the soul and for all harmonic intervals. For there we are repeatedly directed to set out in succession two, three, four, five, or infinitely many sesquialter ratios, or two sesquitertian, sesquiquartan, epogdoic, or whatever superparticular ratios one bids. It is fitting to do this not unskillfully or erroneously, but artfully, unerringly, and swiftly by the following method.
