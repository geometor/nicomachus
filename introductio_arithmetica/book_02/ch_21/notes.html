Chapter 21: Nature of Proportion, Ratio, Continuous & Disjunct Forms — Notes¶

Archival Witnesses & Apparatus¶

  • English Witness: Martin Luther D’Ooge (trans.), Nicomachus of Gerasa: Introduction to Arithmetic (University of Michigan Studies, Vol. XVI, 1926), pp. 264–266 (folios introductio_arithmetica:0280–0282).

  • Greek Witness: Richard Hoche (ed.), Nicomachi Geraseni Pythagorei Introductionis Arithmeticae Libri II (Leipzig: Teubner, 1866), pp. 119–122 (folios hoche_1866:0137–0140).

  • Ancient Parallels: Iamblichus, In Nicomachi Arithmeticam pp. 99–104 Pistelli (iamblichus:pistelli:1894); Boethius, De Institutione Arithmetica II.40 (boethius:friedlein:1867).

Philological & Mathematical Notes¶

  • Analogia vs. Mesotēs: As D'Ooge and Robbins observe (dooge:1926, p. 264 n. 2), earlier writers distinguished analogia (strictly geometric proportion, kata poiotēta, cf. Euclid Elements V Def. 3–6) from mesotēs (“mean”, including arithmetic and harmonic progressions). Nicomachus explicitly allows analogia in the broad sense to cover both ratio-based and difference-based progressions while preserving the strict sense for geometric proportion (Ch. 24.1).

  • Definition of Ratio (*Logos*, II.21.3): Nicomachus defines logos simply as dyo horōn pros allēlous schesis (“relation of two terms to one another”). Compare Euclid (Elements V Def. 3: logos esti dyo megethōn homogenōn hē kata pēlikotēta poia schesis) and Theon of Smyrna (p. 73 Hiller), who specify homogeneous magnitudes and qualitative relation (poia schesis) to distinguish geometric ratio from quantitative arithmetic difference (posē schesis).

  • Continuous (*Synēmmenē*) vs. Disjunct (*Diezeugmenē*): The same distinction appears in Theon of Smyrna (p. 82 Hiller, using synechēs and diērēmenē) and in Boethius (continua vs. disiuncta).
