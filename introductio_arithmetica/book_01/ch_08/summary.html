Chapter 8: The Even-Times Even Number — Summary¶

Overview¶

After opening with the general arithmetic-mean identity \(n = \frac{1}{2}((n-k) + (n+k))\) and proving the primacy of the monad (\(1 = \frac{1}{2}(2)\)), Nicomachus introduces the tripartition of even numbers and analyzes the first extreme species—the even-times even (artiakis artios, powers of two \(2^k\)):

  1. Continuous Bisection to Unity (I.8.4–7): An even-times even number can be halved repeatedly, along with every resulting part, all the way down to the indivisible monad. Every proper divisor and quotient is itself a power of two.

  2. Generation & Reciprocal Divisor Pairing (I.8.8–11): All even-times even numbers are generated exclusively by doubling from unity (\(1, 2, 4, 8, \dots, 2^k\)). In any truncated progression up to \(2^k\), complementary divisors pair symmetrically from the center outward (\(2^j \cdot 2^{k-j} = 2^k\)): two central partners when the term count \(k+1\) is even, or one self-paired square-root mean when \(k+1\) is odd.

  3. Geometric-Series Sum Identity (I.8.12–13): Every partial sum \(1 + 2 + 4 + \dots + 2^{k-1} = 2^k - 1\) is odd and falls short of the next power of two by unity—the key lemma later used in Chapter 16 to generate perfect numbers.

  4. Equal-Products Law (I.8.14): Across symmetric extremes, \(a_0 a_k = a_1 a_{k-1} = \dots\), equaling the product of the two central terms or the square of the single central term.
