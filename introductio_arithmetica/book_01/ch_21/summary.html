Chapter 21: Generation of Superpartients & Overview of Compound Species — Summary¶

Overview¶

Nicomachus constructs the canonical table of superpartient ratios and introduces the transition from the three simple species to the two compound species:

  1. Generating the Root-Forms (I.21.1): Pairing the pure odd numbers from \(5\) (\(5, 7, 9, 11, 13, \dots\)) with all integers from \(3\) (\(3, 4, 5, 6, 7, \dots\)) yields the root-forms (pythmenes) \(5:3, 7:4, 9:5, 11:6, 13:7\), and scaling both terms by \(2, 3, 4, \dots\) fills out each species column.

  2. Implicit Denominators in Greek Nomenclature (I.21.2): Saying epiditritos (“two-thirds”) or epitritetartos (“three-fourths”) automatically supplies the denominator one greater than the numerator of the excess parts (\(1 + \frac{2}{3}, 1 + \frac{3}{4}, 1 + \frac{4}{5}, 1 + \frac{5}{6}\)).

  3. Transition to Compound Relations (I.21.3): Having finished the three simple species, Nicomachus introduces the two species woven out of multiple and fractional excess: multiple superparticular and multiple superpartient, with their sub- reciprocals.
