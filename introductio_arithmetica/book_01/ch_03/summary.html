Chapter 3: The Four Sister Sciences (Mathemata / Quadrivium) — Summary¶

Overview¶

Having delimited finite discrete quantity (to poson) and continuous size (to pēlikon), Nicomachus bifurcates each into two branches to establish the canonical four mathematical sciences (mathemata):

  1. Quantity (*to poson*): * Absolute quantity (kath’ heauto, such as even, odd, perfect): studied by Arithmetic. * Relative quantity (pros allo, such as double, half, hemiolios, epitritos): studied by Music.

  2. Size (*to pēlikon*): * Stationary magnitude (en monēi kai stasei): studied by Geometry. * Moving and revolving magnitude (en kinēsei kai periphorāi): studied by Astronomy (sphairikē).

  3. Ancient Authorities & Pedagogical Ascent (I.3.3–7): Nicomachus cites Androcydes, Archytas (Harmonics DK 47 B 1, calling the four sciences “sisters”), and Plato (Epinomis 991d–992a and Republic VII 527d–e). The four sciences act as “ladders and bridges” (klimakes kai gephyrai) carrying the mind from sensible flux to eternal immaterial truth and rekindling the “eye of the soul.”
