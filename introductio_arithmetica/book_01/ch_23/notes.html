Chapter 23: The Multiple Superpartient & Unfolding All Inequalities from Equality — Notes¶

Archival Witnesses & Apparatus¶

  • English Witness: D’Ooge (1926), pp. 224–229 (folios introductio_arithmetica:0240–0245).

  • Greek Witness: Hoche (1866), pp. 63–72 (folios hoche_1866:0081–0090).

  • Boethius Parallel: Boethius, De Institutione Arithmetica I.30–32 (boethius:friedlein:1867, pp. 62–72 Friedlein).

Editorial & Mathematical Notes¶

  • Algebraic Proof of the Three *Prostagma* Rules: Starting from a geometric progression \((a, b, c) = (x^2, xy, y^2)\) in ratio \(y : x\):

    • In direct order \((x^2, xy, y^2)\), the three rules produce \((x^2,\; x(x+y),\; (x+y)^2)\), which is a geometric progression with ratio \(\frac{x+y}{x} = 1 + \frac{y}{x}\). Starting from \((1, 1, 1)\) (\(x=y=1\)) steps \(\frac{y}{x} = 1 \to 2 \to 3 \to 4 \dots\), generating all multiples \(n : 1\).

    • In reversed order (anastrophe) \((y^2, xy, x^2)\), the three rules produce \((y^2,\; y(y+x),\; (y+x)^2)\), with ratio \(\frac{x+y}{y} = 1 + \frac{x}{y}\). Applying this to reversed multiples \((n^2, n, 1)\) gives \(1 + \frac{1}{n}\) (all superparticulars); applying it to reversed superparticulars gives \(1 + \frac{n}{n+1}\) (superpartients), while direct superparticulars give \(2 + \frac{1}{n}\) (double superparticulars).

  • Scan Verification (Hoche pp. 69–70): Two OCR corruptions in the First1KGreek XML (μδ for με in κε, με, πα and πσ for πα) were verified against Hoche scans 0087 and 0088 and corrected to κε, με, πα (\(25, 45, 81\)).

  • Codex G Scholia (Hoche pp. 71–72): Hoche prints the illustrative numerical tables from Codex G (Göttingen) at the close of Book I (§§8–10); these are preserved at the end of the Greek track.
