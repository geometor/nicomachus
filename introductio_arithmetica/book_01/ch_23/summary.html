Chapter 23: The Multiple Superpartient & Unfolding All Inequalities from Equality — Summary¶

Overview¶

Nicomachus completes the classification of the ten arithmetic relations with the multiple superpartient (pollaplasiepimerēs), and then crowns Book I with the generative theorem deriving all species of inequality from Equality:

  1. Multiple Superpartient (I.23.1–3): The greater term contains the lesser more than once plus more than one aliquot part of the lesser (\(n + \frac{p}{q}\) with \(n \ge 2, 1 < p < q\)), yielding double superbipartient (\(8:3\)), double supertripartient (\(11:4\)), triple superbipartient (\(11:3\)), and their sub- reciprocals.

  2. Cosmological Significance of Equality (I.23.4–5): Just as determinate form bounds the indeterminate in nature and reason orders passion and appetite into ethical virtues, so primordial equality is the mother and root from which every species of inequality unfolds.

  3. The Three *Prostagma* Rules (I.23.6–17): Starting from any three equal terms \((a, a, a)\)—such as (1, 1, 1)—and forming the new triple \((a,\; a+b,\; a+2b+c)\) yields:

    • First, all multiples in order: doubles (1, 2, 4), triples (1, 3, 9), quadruples (1, 4, 16), etc.

    • Second, reversing the multiples (anastrophe) and applying the same three rules generates all superparticulars: (4, 6, 9) sesquialters, (9, 12, 16) sesquitertians, etc.

    • Third, keeping superparticulars in direct order yields multiple superparticulars ((4, 10, 25)), while reversing superparticulars yields superpartients ((9, 15, 25)), and applying the rules to superpartients in direct and reversed order generates multiple superpartients ((9, 24, 64)) and higher superpartients—every triple having square extremes and a mean equal to the product of their sides.
