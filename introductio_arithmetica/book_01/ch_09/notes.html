Chapter 9: The Even-Times Odd Number — Notes¶

Archival Witnesses & Apparatus¶

  • English Witness: D’Ooge (1926), pp. 196–198 (folios introductio_arithmetica:0212–0214).

  • Greek Witness: Hoche (1866), pp. 19–21 (folios hoche_1866:0037–0039).

  • Boethius Parallel: Boethius, De Institutione Arithmetica I.10 (boethius:friedlein:1867, pp. 21–24 Friedlein).

Editorial & Mathematical Notes¶

  • Exclusion of 2 (I.9.1–4): Because Nicomachus generates even-times odd numbers by doubling the odd numbers starting from \(3\) (excluding the monad as a number), \(2\) is classified strictly with the even-times even (\(2^1\)) rather than as \(2 \times 1\); contrast Euclid, Elements VII, Def. 9 (euclid:elements:vii-ix).

  • Arithmetic vs. Geometric Mean Contrast (I.9.6): The contrast between multiplicative pairings in Ch. 8 (\(2^j \cdot 2^{k-j} = 2^k\)) and additive pairings in Ch. 9 (\((4a+2) + (4b+2) = 2 \cdot (2a+2b+2)\)) anticipates Book II’s systematic study of arithmetic and geometric proportions (analogia).
