Unit I — Philosophy, the Quadrivium, the Odd Sieve & Perfect Numbers — Structural Digest¶

Argumentative Architecture & Interactive Diagram Guide · Book I, Chs. 1–7, 13, 16

Architectural Thesis¶

Unit I establishes the philosophical charter of the Western mathematical curriculum before classifying odd primes and even perfect numbers:

  1. Immaterial Reality & the Dichotomy of Quantity (I.1–2): Following Pythagoras and Plato's Timaeus (plato:timaeus:27d-36d), true wisdom studies eternal immaterial realities rather than bodily flux. Quantity bifurcates into discontinuous Multitude (poson, bounded below by the monad and increasing without limit) and continuous Magnitude (pelikon, bounded as a whole and infinitely divisible).

  2. The Four Sister Sciences & Asymmetric Priority (I.3–6): Quoting Archytas (archytas:dk47b1), Nicomachus maps absolute vs. relative multitude to Arithmetic and Music, and stationary vs. revolving magnitude to Geometry and Astronomy. By asymmetric co-removal, Arithmetic precedes all three sister sciences and exists pre-eminently as the cosmic exemplar in the Mind of the Creator (I.6.1).

  3. The Odd Sieve of Eratosthenes & Perfect Numbers (I.7, 13, 16): Operating strictly on odd numbers \(\ge 3\), Eratosthenes's Sieve steps by \(p\) positions along the odd sequence (\(2p\) in integers) to separate primes from odd composites, while Euclid's formula \(2^{p-1}(2^p - 1)\) generates the perfect numbers \(6, 28, 496, 8128\).
