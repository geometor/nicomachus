Chapter 6: Number as Cosmic Exemplar in the Mind of the Creator¶

1. Everything in the cosmos that has been arranged by nature according to craftsmanship—both in its parts and as a whole—manifestly stands distinguished and adorned according to number by Providence and by the Mind that crafted the universe. This exemplar was established like a preliminary sketch because number subsisted beforehand in the thought of the world-fashioning God: purely intelligible and altogether immaterial, yet truly eternal substance, so that in accordance with it, as an artistic pattern, all these things might be brought to completion—time, motion, heaven, the stars, and revolutions of every kind.

2. It is necessary, therefore, that scientific number, already presiding over such things, be harmonized in itself—and not by another, but by itself.

f. 0206 · p. 190

3. Now every harmonized thing is necessarily harmonized out of opposites, and indeed out of things that actually exist. For neither things that do not exist can be harmonized, nor things that exist yet are wholly alike to one another, nor things that differ yet lack any ratio toward one another. It remains, then, that the elements out of which a thing is harmonized must be existent, distinct, and possessed of a ratio to one another.

4. Out of such elements, therefore, scientific number also consists. For its two primary species—having the substance of quantity, differing from one another, yet not alien in kind—are the odd and the even; and these are harmonized together alternately by a wondrous and divine nature, inseparably and in unified form, as we shall presently see.
