Chapter 20: Third Species of Inequality: The Superpartient — Summary¶

Overview¶

Nicomachus defines the third species of greater inequality, the superpartient (epimeres), together with its correlative, the subsuperpartient (hypepimerēs):

  1. Definition (I.20.1, 3): A number is superpartient when it contains the whole of the lesser number plus more than one aliquot part of it (\((n + r) : n\) with \(1 < r < n\) and \(\gcd(n, r) = 1\))—specifically superbipartient (2 extra parts), supertripartient (3 extra parts), superquadripartient (4 extra parts), etc.

  2. Why the Parts Begin at Thirds and Odd Denominators (I.20.2): Two halves added to the whole would yield a double (a multiple, \(2:1\)), while two fourths equal a half and two sixths equal a third (reducing to superparticulars). Thus the root superbipartient series necessarily begins at \(\frac{2}{3}\) (\(5:3\)) and advances through odd denominators \(\frac{2}{5}\) (\(7:5\)), \(\frac{2}{7}\) (\(9:7\)), \(\frac{2}{9}\) (\(11:9\)).
