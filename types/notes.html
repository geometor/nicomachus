Unit I — Philosophy, the Quadrivium, the Odd Sieve & Perfect Numbers — Notes

Poson vs. Pēlikon and Aristotle’s Categories: In aristotle:cat:4b20 (Categories 6, 4b20), Aristotle divided quantity (to poson) into discrete (number and speech) and continuous (line, surface, body, time, place). Nicomachus refines the terminology by reserving poson (multitudo) strictly for bounded discrete multitude and pelikon (magnitudo) for bounded continuous magnitude. Five centuries later, Proclus reproduces Nicomachus’s fourfold schema verbatim in his Prologue to Euclid (proclus:in-euclid:35-37, pp. 35.17–36.25 Friedlein), while Boethius renders Nicomachus’s “four methods” (tessares methodoi), “bridges” (gephyrai), and “ladders” (klimakes) by the newly minted Latin compound quadruvium (“fourfold crossroads”) at De Institutione Arithmetica I.1 (boethius:friedlein:1867, p. 7.25).

Unit II — Five Species of Inequality & the Equality Unfolding Tree — Notes

Nicomachus observes with wonder at the close of Book I (I.23.16, Hoche p. 71; D’Ooge p. 229) that in every triple generated from Equality (1, 1, 1), the first and third terms are squares while the middle term is their geometric mean. Exact algebra reveals why Nicomachus’s three prostagmata (attributed by Iamblichus [iamblichus:pistelli:1894] and Theon of Smyrna [theon:hiller:1878, p. 107] to Eratosthenes and Adrastus) are nothing less than the symmetric square representation \(\mathrm{Sym}^2(\mathbb{Z}^2)\) of the Euclidean unimodular group \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0})\)!
